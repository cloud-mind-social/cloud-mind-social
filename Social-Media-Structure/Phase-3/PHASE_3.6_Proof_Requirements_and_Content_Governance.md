# Phase 3.6 — Proof Requirements and Content Governance

**Status:** In Progress  
**Phase:** 3 — Content Architecture  
**Dependency:** Phase 3.5 CTA Framework

## Purpose

Define the evidence, proof and governance requirements CMS must apply before content is approved for public use.

CMS is in an early commercial stage. The content system must build trust without confusing proof of method, market research, strategic interpretation and verified client results.

## Proof principle

CMS content must be as confident as the evidence allows.

**Evidence → Interpretation → Claim → CTA**

If the evidence is limited, the claim must be limited. If the claim is limited, the CTA must remain proportionate.

## Evidence classes

### Class A — Approved strategic decision

A decision explicitly approved by CMS's owner and treated as the current strategic baseline.

**Examples:**

- Primary ICP
- Western-first commercial priority
- Diagnosis before prescription
- Strategic Social Intelligence
- Current platform scope

**Use:** May be stated as CMS's position.

**Requirement:** Reference the approved source and do not present it as independent market proof.

### Class B — Direct CMS evidence

Evidence from CMS's own verified work, conversations, delivery records, tests or client outcomes.

**Examples:**

- Documented prospect language
- Paid pilot results
- Verified delivery hours
- Permissioned client outcome
- Recorded acquisition response
- Actual objection pattern

**Use:** May support specific CMS claims when accurately represented.

**Requirement:** Preserve source, date, context, permission and limitations.

### Class C — Research-supported market evidence

Evidence from approved external research, credible market sources or Track A findings.

**Examples:**

- Market-level agency-client patterns
- Pricing and budget observations
- Public competitor evidence
- Approved Track A findings
- Research-supported buyer-risk patterns

**Use:** May support qualified market observations.

**Requirement:** State the scope of the evidence. Do not present market-level evidence as direct proof about CMS prospects.

### Class D — Strategic interpretation

A reasoned CMS interpretation based on approved decisions and evidence.

**Examples:**

- A likely content implication
- A proposed buyer journey
- A recommended CTA route
- A strategic explanation of why a service may fit

**Use:** Present as analysis, interpretation or recommendation.

**Requirement:** Do not present interpretation as observed buyer behavior.

### Class E — Working hypothesis

A claim CMS intends to test.

**Examples:**

- Diagnostic-first messaging will improve qualified interest.
- A Social Signal Check will generate useful problem language.
- A specific CTA will produce better-fit conversations.

**Use:** Use in experiments and internal planning.

**Requirement:** Label as a hypothesis. Do not publish it as a proven result.

### Class F — Unverified claim

A claim without sufficient evidence, attribution or source clarity.

**Use:** Exclude from formal CMS content.

**Examples:**

- Guaranteed lead or revenue outcomes
- Unverified client results
- Unsupported statements about what all business owners want
- Claims that CMS is “trusted by” unnamed clients
- Claims based only on a research question

## Proof hierarchy

From strongest to weakest:

1. Permissioned client outcome with context and timeframe
2. Named testimonial with role, business context and project relevance
3. Verified case study
4. Documented paid pilot or acquisition evidence
5. Direct prospect language or recorded objection pattern
6. Public work sample
7. Proof-of-method demonstration
8. Approved external research
9. Strategic interpretation
10. Working hypothesis
11. Unverified claim

The proof hierarchy determines how strongly a claim may be expressed. It does not mean weaker evidence is useless; it means weaker evidence requires narrower language.

## Proof type separation

### Proof of method

Demonstrates how CMS thinks, investigates, prioritizes or recommends.

**May include:**

- Sample diagnostic
- Annotated decision brief
- Hypothetical scenario
- Research-to-decision walkthrough
- Process explanation
- Mock recommendation

**Must say:** This demonstrates the method.

**Must not say:** This proves client results.

### Proof of capability

Demonstrates that CMS can perform a defined task or make a defined type of decision.

**May include:**

- Portfolio work
- Content or profile analysis
- Strategic framework
- Platform knowledge
- Documented process
- Relevant professional experience

**Must say:** What CMS did and under what conditions.

**Must not imply:** A capability automatically produces a guaranteed outcome.

### Client proof

Demonstrates genuine work with a real client.

**Must include where possible:**

- Client or approved anonymized context
- Business problem
- Trigger or objective
- CMS role
- Decisions made
- Deliverables
- Timeframe
- Evidence
- Constraints
- Permission and attribution

**Must not include:** Fabricated, borrowed, unverifiable or context-free results.

### Market proof

Demonstrates a broader pattern from credible external evidence.

**Must include:**

- Source
- Date or research period
- Scope
- Relevant limitation
- CMS interpretation separated from source findings

**Must not include:** The suggestion that market evidence confirms CMS's individual buyer behavior.

## Claim register

Before publishing a significant claim, record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Claim | Exact wording or close draft |
| Claim type | Strategic decision, direct evidence, market evidence, interpretation, hypothesis or unverified |
| Source | File, conversation, test, client record or external reference |
| Date | When the evidence was collected or approved |
| Scope | Who or what the claim applies to |
| Confidence | 🟢 High, 🟡 Moderate, 🟠 Anecdotal or 🔴 Unverified |
| Limitation | What the evidence does not prove |
| Approval | Person responsible for approval |
| Review date | When the claim should be rechecked |

## Content approval workflow

### Step 1 — Strategic fit

Confirm the content:

- Serves one Phase 3.2 pillar.
- Expresses one theme and angle.
- Supports CMS's approved positioning.
- Has a clear audience realization.

### Step 2 — Evidence check

Confirm:

- What claim is being made.
- What evidence supports it.
- Whether the source is direct, market-level or interpretive.
- Whether the wording matches the evidence strength.
- Whether the evidence has a relevant limitation.

### Step 3 — Proof labeling

Label the content internally as:

- Approved strategic position
- Proof of method
- Proof of capability
- Client proof
- Market interpretation
- Working hypothesis
- Unverified and excluded

### Step 4 — Funnel and CTA check

Confirm:

- The funnel stage is appropriate.
- The CTA matches audience readiness.
- The destination exists or is marked pending.
- The CTA does not imply unsupported results.
- The content does not skip diagnosis where diagnosis is necessary.

### Step 5 — Risk review

Check for:

- Unsupported superlatives
- Guaranteed outcomes
- Misleading statistics
- Unclear attribution
- Missing permission
- Confidential client details
- False urgency
- Competitor attacks
- Ambiguous AI-generated claims
- Inaccurate use of “case study,” “client result” or “testimonial”

### Step 6 — Final approval

Record:

- Approver
- Date
- Evidence class
- Intended platform adaptation
- Required disclosure
- Review trigger

## Content risk levels

### Low-risk content

- CMS point of view
- Educational explanation
- Clearly labeled framework
- General process explanation
- Conversation prompt

**Approval:** Strategic fit and evidence check.

### Medium-risk content

- Market statistics
- Research interpretation
- Pricing observations
- Objection responses
- Competitive comparisons
- Proof-of-method examples

**Approval:** Strategic fit, evidence check, limitation and source review.

### High-risk content

- Client results
- Testimonials
- Case studies
- Revenue, lead or ROI claims
- Public pricing promises
- Guarantees
- Legal, regulatory or disclosure-sensitive claims

**Approval:** Full claim register, permission or source verification and explicit final approval.

## Governance rules

- Do not use a research question as evidence.
- Do not use a theoretical assumption as a client result.
- Do not describe a hypothetical example as a case study.
- Do not use a testimonial without permission and attribution rules.
- Do not publish statistics without source, date and scope.
- Do not use “trusted by,” “proven,” “guaranteed” or similar language without defensible support.
- Do not hide limitations that materially change how a claim should be understood.
- Do not let AI-generated content invent clients, outcomes, quotes or sources.
- Do not use competitor claims as independently verified facts unless CMS has a reliable source.
- Do not make a CTA stronger than the evidence and audience readiness justify.

## Phase 3 content governance checklist

Before approval:

- [ ] Pillar and theme are identified.
- [ ] Audience realization is clear.
- [ ] Funnel stage is identified.
- [ ] CTA and destination are defined.
- [ ] Claim type is labeled.
- [ ] Evidence source is recorded.
- [ ] Confidence level is recorded.
- [ ] Limitations are documented.
- [ ] Proof type is not misrepresented.
- [ ] Permission and attribution are confirmed where necessary.
- [ ] No unsupported outcome claim is present.
- [ ] Content does not prematurely sell an ongoing service.
- [ ] Platform adaptation can occur later without changing the strategic claim.
- [ ] Required reviewer has approved the content.

## Governance ownership

| Responsibility | Owner |
|---|---|
| Strategic fit | CMS strategy lead |
| Evidence and claim classification | CMS strategy lead |
| Client permission and attribution | Account or client owner |
| Scope and offer accuracy | Commercial or delivery owner |
| Platform adaptation | Platform content owner in Phase 5 |
| Final public approval | CMS owner or explicitly delegated approver |

## Measurement and correction

If published content produces:

- Confusion about who CMS serves
- Misunderstanding of the offer
- Objections caused by unclear claims
- Evidence challenges
- Unqualified demand
- Misleading interpretation
- A mismatch between promise and delivery

CMS should record the issue, correct the content where necessary and update the relevant claim, assumption or governance rule.

## Definition of done

Phase 3.6 is complete when:

- Evidence classes are defined.
- Proof types are separated.
- Claim strength rules are documented.
- A claim register structure exists.
- Content approval workflow is clear.
- Risk levels and governance rules are documented.
- CTA and evidence controls work together.
- The framework is ready to support Phase 3 completion and later Phase 4 or Phase 5 work.
