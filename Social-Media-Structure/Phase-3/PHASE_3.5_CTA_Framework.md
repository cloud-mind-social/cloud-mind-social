# Phase 3.5 — CTA Framework

**Status:** In Progress  
**Phase:** 3 — Content Architecture  
**Dependency:** Phase 3.4 Content-to-Funnel Mapping

## Purpose

Define how CMS uses calls to action to guide people toward the next appropriate step without forcing every audience member into a sales conversation.

A CTA should clarify what the audience can do next, why that action is relevant and what level of commitment it requires.

## CTA principle

CMS uses CTAs to create useful movement:

**Understand → Reflect → Identify → Explore → Diagnose → Decide → Continue**

The CTA must match the audience's current readiness. It should not ask for a level of commitment the content has not earned.

## CTA categories

### 1. Attention CTA

**Purpose:** Encourage continued exposure to CMS thinking.

**Audience state:** Unaware or lightly aware.

**Examples:**

- Follow CMS for more practical breakdowns.
- Save this for your next marketing review.
- Share this with the person who owns your social direction.
- Keep this distinction in mind before changing your content plan.

**Destination:** Social profile, saved content or shared content.

**Do not use when:** The audience is already ready for a specific diagnostic action.

### 2. Reflection CTA

**Purpose:** Help the audience compare the content with their own situation.

**Audience state:** Problem-aware.

**Examples:**

- Which part of this feels familiar in your business?
- What are you currently treating as a content problem?
- What decision remains unclear?
- Where is ownership breaking down?

**Destination:** Comments, replies, private conversation or internal reflection.

**Commercial role:** Generates problem language and early intent signals.

### 3. Self-assessment CTA

**Purpose:** Help the audience identify a possible constraint through a bounded action.

**Audience state:** Problem-aware or constraint-aware.

**Examples:**

- Complete the Social Signal Check.
- Use these questions to assess your current setup.
- Compare your current process against this checklist.
- Identify whether the issue is capacity, capability, coordination or measurement.

**Destination:** Social Signal Check, checklist or resource.

**Commercial role:** Moves recognition toward structured understanding.

### 4. Education CTA

**Purpose:** Direct the audience toward a deeper explanation.

**Audience state:** Problem-aware or constraint-aware.

**Examples:**

- Read the full breakdown.
- Review how this decision should be made.
- Explore the difference between the stated problem and the underlying constraint.
- Learn what to examine before hiring support.

**Destination:** Website insight, resource or educational page.

**Commercial role:** Builds depth without premature selling.

### 5. Conversation CTA

**Purpose:** Invite relevant context, questions or audience language.

**Audience state:** Problem-aware, trust-building or relationship-oriented.

**Examples:**

- Describe what is currently happening in your business.
- Tell us what you have already tried.
- Ask the question you are stuck on.
- Share the part of your process that feels fragmented.

**Destination:** Comments, replies or permission-based direct conversation.

**Commercial role:** Supports audience learning and relationship-led acquisition.

**Guardrail:** A conversation CTA must have a defined response and routing process.

### 6. Method CTA

**Purpose:** Let the audience inspect how CMS approaches a problem.

**Audience state:** Trust-building or consideration.

**Examples:**

- Review how CMS approaches diagnosis.
- See what we examine before recommending execution.
- Explore the process behind the recommendation.
- Review a proof-of-method example.

**Destination:** Method page, process explanation, sample decision brief or diagnostic overview.

**Commercial role:** Reduces perceived risk by making the method visible.

### 7. Review CTA

**Purpose:** Move a specific, qualified problem toward a bounded review.

**Audience state:** Intent-ready.

**Examples:**

- Request a Signal-to-Strategy Review.
- Bring the specific problem you are trying to solve.
- Ask CMS to review the current situation and identify the next decision.
- Submit the context needed for a focused review.

**Destination:** Signal-to-Strategy Review form or qualification flow.

**Commercial role:** Converts specific interest into a qualified diagnostic opportunity.

### 8. Diagnostic CTA

**Purpose:** Invite a paid or formally bounded diagnosis.

**Audience state:** Diagnosis-ready.

**Examples:**

- Book the Social Clarity Intensive.
- Start with a finite diagnosis before committing to ongoing support.
- Receive a focused decision brief for the next step.
- Confirm whether a deeper engagement is appropriate.

**Destination:** Social Clarity Intensive page, booking flow or proposal pathway.

**Commercial role:** Tests willingness to pay and prevents premature retainer selling.

### 9. Service CTA

**Purpose:** Move a diagnosed need into the correct delivery pathway.

**Audience state:** Post-diagnosis and qualified.

**Examples:**

- Review the recommended À La Carte scope.
- Begin the appropriate Presence, Momentum, Authority or Growth Partner engagement.
- Approve the next phase of support.
- Start the defined implementation scope.

**Destination:** Service page, proposal, contract or client onboarding pathway.

**Commercial role:** Converts a diagnosed need into right-sized support.

**Guardrail:** Do not use this as the default CTA for cold content.

### 10. Continuation CTA

**Purpose:** Support renewal, expansion, review or referral after work has begun.

**Audience state:** Existing client or verified advocate.

**Examples:**

- Review the next constraint.
- Continue the current support scope.
- Expand only where the next need justifies it.
- Share a testimonial or referral with permission.
- Review what changed and what should happen next.

**Destination:** Client review, renewal, expansion or referral process.

**Commercial role:** Supports retention and appropriate growth without manufactured urgency.

## CTA readiness ladder

| Audience readiness | Primary CTA type | Appropriate destination |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Attention | Social profile or content |
| Lightly aware | Attention; Reflection | Social content or conversation |
| Problem-aware | Reflection; Self-assessment; Education | Social Signal Check or resource |
| Constraint-aware | Self-assessment; Method; Conversation | Resource, method page or review request |
| Trust-building | Method; Education; Proof | Website method, proof or offer page |
| Intent-ready | Review; FAQ; Objection response | Signal-to-Strategy Review |
| Diagnosis-ready | Diagnostic | Social Clarity Intensive |
| Qualified and diagnosed | Service | À La Carte or core service pathway |
| Existing client | Continuation | Review, renewal, expansion or referral |

## CTA-to-funnel mapping

| Funnel stage | Audience need | CTA priority | Commitment level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Notice and name the problem | Attention; Reflection | Low |
| Realization | Understand the possible constraint | Self-assessment; Education | Low to moderate |
| Trust | Evaluate CMS's method and credibility | Method; Proof; Conversation | Moderate |
| Intent | Decide whether CMS may be relevant | Review; FAQ; Objection response | Moderate |
| Diagnostic conversation | Share context and examine the situation | Diagnostic | Moderate to high |
| Recommendation | Approve an appropriate next step | Service | High |
| Renewal or expansion | Continue based on evidence | Continuation | High but justified |

## CTA construction formula

A strong CMS CTA should contain:

1. **Action:** What should the person do?
2. **Reason:** Why is that action relevant now?
3. **Boundary:** What will happen, and what will not happen?
4. **Destination:** Where should the person go?
5. **Readiness fit:** Why is this appropriate for their current state?

### Example

**Weak:** “Book a call today.”

**Stronger:** “If your social activity is active but disconnected from a clear business decision, request a Signal-to-Strategy Review. We will examine the stated problem, identify the likely constraint and clarify the next step before discussing ongoing support.”

## CTA language rules

Use language that is:

- Clear
- Specific
- Proportionate
- Permission-based
- Connected to the content
- Honest about the next step
- Free from manufactured urgency

Avoid:

- “Book now before it’s too late.”
- “Guaranteed results.”
- “Get more leads instantly.”
- “DM us for a free full strategy.”
- “Only three slots left” unless factually true and appropriate.
- “Let us handle everything” without defining scope.
- “Buy this package” before diagnosis where diagnosis is necessary.

## CTA routing rules

- A recognition post should not usually route directly to a retainer.
- A self-assessment should lead to a bounded next step, not an automatic sales call.
- A method or proof asset may route to a review when the problem is specific.
- A review should route to the diagnostic or the appropriate next decision, not automatically to ongoing management.
- A diagnostic should produce a recommendation proportionate to the actual need.
- A service CTA belongs after qualification or diagnosis unless the À La Carte scope is already clear and finite.
- A continuation CTA should be based on documented learning, not pressure.

## CTA destination architecture

| CTA destination | What it should contain | Primary CTA types |
|---|---|---|
| Social profile | Category clarity, point of view and next low-risk action | Attention; Reflection |
| Social Signal Check | Bounded questions and problem categories | Self-assessment |
| Insight or resource page | Deeper explanation and related next step | Education; Method |
| Method page | CMS process, boundaries and proof of thinking | Method |
| Signal-to-Strategy Review | Qualification, context and review expectations | Review |
| Social Clarity Intensive | Scope, output, price or pricing logic and boundaries | Diagnostic |
| À La Carte page | Finite deliverable, timeline, requirements and exclusions | Service |
| Core service page | Who it fits, scope, responsibilities and next step | Service |
| Client pathway | Proposal, contract, payment, onboarding and approvals | Continuation; Service |

## CTA measurement

Measure the quality of movement, not only clicks:

- Attention; relevant follows, saves and return visits
- Reflection; meaningful replies and useful problem language
- Self-assessment; completion rate and quality of identified problems
- Education; resource engagement and repeated visits
- Method; proof interaction and clarity questions
- Review; qualified submissions and problem specificity
- Diagnostic; attendance, willingness to pay and decision clarity
- Service; scope acceptance, fit, delivery effort and margin
- Continuation; renewal, expansion, referral and documented progress

A high click-through rate does not prove that the CTA is commercially effective if the resulting actions are unqualified or misrouted.

## Governance checklist

Before approving a CTA, confirm:

- [ ] The audience state is identified.
- [ ] The CTA matches the funnel stage.
- [ ] The requested commitment is proportionate.
- [ ] The destination exists or is explicitly marked as pending.
- [ ] The response or routing process is defined.
- [ ] The CTA does not promise unsupported outcomes.
- [ ] The CTA does not bypass diagnosis where diagnosis is needed.
- [ ] The CTA does not make every audience member look sales-ready.
- [ ] The CTA reflects CMS's diagnosis-before-prescription positioning.
- [ ] The CTA can be adapted to each platform later without changing its strategic intent.

## Definition of done

Phase 3.5 is complete when:

- CTA types are defined.
- CTA readiness levels are documented.
- Funnel-stage routing is clear.
- Destinations are mapped.
- CTA construction and language rules are documented.
- Measurement criteria distinguish attention from qualified movement.
- Governance prevents premature or misleading sales CTAs.
- The framework is ready for Phase 3.6 proof requirements and governance integration.
