# Phase 3.3 — Content Formats and Strategic Roles

**Status:** In Progress  
**Phase:** 3 — Content Architecture  
**Dependency:** Phase 3.2 Content Pillar and Strategic Theme Architecture

## Purpose

Define the strategic role of each content format before CMS adapts content to specific platforms.

A format is not a content pillar. It is the delivery structure used to express a pillar, theme, angle, audience realization, funnel role and CTA.

This document does not define platform dimensions, posting cadence, editing rules or platform-specific execution. Those belong in Phase 5.

## Format selection model

Every content asset should answer:

1. Which Phase 3.2 pillar does it serve?
2. Which strategic theme does it express?
3. What audience realization should it create?
4. What funnel role does it perform?
5. What evidence supports the claim?
6. What CTA matches the audience's readiness?
7. Why is this format appropriate for this idea?

Do not choose a format merely because it is popular or easy to produce.

# Core content formats and roles

## 1. Strategic Point of View

**Role:** Make CMS's beliefs and judgment visible.

**Best for:**

- Diagnosis before prescription
- Business before content
- Strategic Social Intelligence
- Misconceptions and industry tensions
- Clear distinctions between CMS and generic providers

**Primary funnel role:** Recognition, realization and trust

**Typical CTA:** Reflect, respond, save or follow the ongoing point of view.

**Risk:** Becoming unsupported opinion or founder commentary without commercial relevance.

## 2. Diagnostic Breakdown

**Role:** Help the audience identify the difference between a visible symptom and an underlying constraint.

**Best for:**

- Stated problem versus actual problem
- Fragmented social activity
- Capacity, coordination, capability and measurement issues
- Outgrown-DIY triggers
- Wrong-fix patterns

**Primary funnel role:** Recognition and realization

**Typical CTA:** Complete the Social Signal Check or describe the current situation.

**Risk:** Giving away a complete diagnosis without context or turning into generic audit content.

## 3. Educational Explanation

**Role:** Clarify a complex idea in plain language.

**Best for:**

- Explaining marketing decisions
- Defining unfamiliar concepts
- Showing how social media connects to business context
- Teaching the audience what to examine before execution

**Primary funnel role:** Recognition, realization and trust

**Typical CTA:** Save, share, ask a question or explore the related method.

**Risk:** Becoming generic educational content that could belong to any agency.

## 4. Decision Framework

**Role:** Give the audience a structured way to make a better marketing decision.

**Best for:**

- Choosing between DIY, AI, VA, freelancer, agency or in-house support
- Deciding whether a platform is appropriate
- Determining whether a problem is strategic or executional
- Selecting the right depth of support
- Prioritizing next steps

**Primary funnel role:** Realization, trust and consideration

**Typical CTA:** Apply the framework or request a Signal-to-Strategy Review.

**Risk:** Oversimplifying decisions that require diagnosis.

## 5. Checklist or Self-Assessment

**Role:** Create low-friction participation and help the audience name a problem.

**Best for:**

- Social Signal Check prompts
- Readiness questions
- Diagnostic symptoms
- Pre-consultation reflection
- Self-identification of capacity or coordination problems

**Primary funnel role:** Recognition and intent

**Typical CTA:** Complete the checklist, share the result or request the next diagnostic step.

**Risk:** Attracting low-intent engagement without qualification.

## 6. Process Walkthrough

**Role:** Make CMS's method, responsibilities and decision sequence visible.

**Best for:**

- Discovery and diagnosis
- How recommendations are made
- How scope follows the diagnosed need
- What happens before execution
- Client approvals and communication
- What CMS will not recommend without justification

**Primary funnel role:** Trust and consideration

**Typical CTA:** Review the diagnostic pathway or request a bounded conversation.

**Risk:** Describing internal process without showing why it matters to the buyer.

## 7. Decision-Trace Case

**Role:** Demonstrate how a problem is interpreted and how a recommendation follows from evidence.

**Best for:**

- Proof of method
- Before-and-after reasoning
- Hypothetical or anonymized scenarios
- Lessons from CMS testing
- Showing why one option was selected over another

**Primary funnel role:** Trust and intent

**Typical CTA:** Inspect the method, compare the situation or request a review.

**Risk:** Presenting hypothetical work as client proof. All hypothetical or anonymized examples must be labelled accurately.

## 8. Research Interpretation

**Role:** Translate market or audience evidence into a useful business implication.

**Best for:**

- Track A findings
- Approved research-supported assumptions
- AI and market changes
- Pricing and buyer-risk observations
- Industry behavior patterns

**Primary funnel role:** Trust and authority

**Typical CTA:** Consider the implication, discuss the finding or review the related CMS position.

**Risk:** Treating market-level research as direct proof about CMS prospects.

## 9. Objection and Alternative Response

**Role:** Address the concern behind a buying hesitation or alternative solution.

**Best for:**

- “I can do this myself.”
- “I only need someone to post.”
- “I tried a freelancer already.”
- “Why do I need strategy?”
- “Why does this cost more than a VA?”
- “Can’t AI handle this?”

**Primary funnel role:** Consideration and intent

**Typical CTA:** Compare the situation, ask a specific question or request the appropriate diagnostic.

**Risk:** Becoming defensive, attacking competitors or answering objections CMS has not actually observed.

## 10. Proof-of-Method Demonstration

**Role:** Show the quality of CMS's thinking without claiming client outcomes.

**Best for:**

- Sample audits
- Annotated decision briefs
- Example diagnosis
- Mock recommendations
- Research-to-decision demonstrations
- Content or profile analysis with transparent limitations

**Primary funnel role:** Trust

**Typical CTA:** Review the method or request a bounded diagnostic.

**Risk:** Confusing proof of method with proof of results.

## 11. Verified Case Study

**Role:** Demonstrate genuine client work and documented outcomes.

**Best for:**

- Client context
- Problem and trigger
- CMS role
- Decisions made
- Deliverables
- Evidence and timeframe
- Limitations
- Next step

**Primary funnel role:** Trust, intent and conversion

**Typical CTA:** Discuss a comparable situation or begin the appropriate diagnostic.

**Risk:** Publishing results without permission, context, timeframe or attribution.

## 12. Conversation Prompt

**Role:** Invite the audience to share language, experience or perspective.

**Best for:**

- Audience learning
- Founder and operator discussions
- Problem-language discovery
- Exploring how people describe fragmentation or urgency
- Building relationship-led trust

**Primary funnel role:** Recognition and trust

**Typical CTA:** Reply, describe the situation or compare experiences.

**Risk:** Collecting engagement without connecting the learning to CMS's strategy or commercial understanding.

## 13. Founder or Strategist Reflection

**Role:** Add a human perspective to CMS's strategic point of view.

**Best for:**

- Lessons from building CMS
- Decisions changed by evidence
- Honest limitations
- Why CMS works in a particular way
- Personal observations connected to the buyer's reality

**Primary funnel role:** Recognition and trust

**Typical CTA:** Follow the thinking, respond or explore the related method.

**Risk:** Becoming personal content with no connection to CMS's audience or commercial role.

## 14. FAQ or Clarification

**Role:** Reduce confusion about CMS, the process, the offers or the next step.

**Best for:**

- Who CMS serves
- What CMS does
- What CMS does not do
- How diagnostics work
- What clients provide
- How scope and approvals work
- When a service is not appropriate

**Primary funnel role:** Trust, consideration and intent

**Typical CTA:** Ask a specific question or choose the appropriate entry point.

**Risk:** Creating FAQs from assumptions rather than real confusion or observed objections.

# Format-to-funnel mapping

| Funnel stage | Strongest formats | Strategic purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Strategic Point of View; Diagnostic Breakdown; Educational Explanation; Conversation Prompt | Help the audience notice and name a problem |
| Realization | Diagnostic Breakdown; Decision Framework; Checklist; Research Interpretation | Help the audience understand what may actually be happening |
| Trust | Process Walkthrough; Proof-of-Method Demonstration; Founder Reflection; Research Interpretation | Show how CMS thinks, works and handles uncertainty |
| Intent | Decision Framework; Objection Response; FAQ; Decision-Trace Case | Reduce risk and make the next decision easier |
| Diagnostic conversation | Checklist; Process Walkthrough; Proof-of-Method Demonstration; FAQ | Route qualified interest toward the right diagnostic |
| Right-sized support | Verified Case Study; Process Walkthrough; Decision-Trace Case; FAQ | Support a recommendation after diagnosis |

# Format selection rules

Choose the format based on the strategic job:

- Use a **Strategic Point of View** when CMS needs to clarify what it believes.
- Use a **Diagnostic Breakdown** when the audience is misidentifying the problem.
- Use an **Educational Explanation** when the audience lacks a concept.
- Use a **Decision Framework** when the audience faces alternatives.
- Use a **Checklist** when the audience needs a low-friction first step.
- Use a **Process Walkthrough** when uncertainty about working with CMS is the barrier.
- Use a **Proof-of-Method Demonstration** when CMS needs credibility but lacks client proof.
- Use a **Verified Case Study** only when genuine evidence and permission exist.
- Use an **Objection Response** when a recurring, documented concern needs clarification.
- Use a **Conversation Prompt** when audience language or relationship learning is the goal.

# Format governance

Before approving a format, confirm:

- The format serves one primary strategic purpose.
- The format is connected to a Phase 3.2 pillar and theme.
- The idea does not rely on unsupported claims.
- The CTA matches audience readiness.
- The format does not accidentally become a platform strategy.
- The content can be adapted later without changing its strategic meaning.
- The evidence level is visible to the person producing or approving it.
- The format does not duplicate another format without a clear reason.

# Content-format exclusions

Do not create a format merely for:

- Filling a posting quota
- Following a trend
- Producing more assets
- Maximizing surface-level engagement
- Copying a competitor
- Making CMS appear active without a strategic purpose
- Hiding the absence of proof
- Making a sales claim sound more credible than the evidence allows

# Definition of done

Phase 3.3 is complete when:

- Core formats have distinct strategic roles.
- Each format is connected to a pillar, theme, funnel stage and CTA.
- Proof-of-method and client-proof formats are clearly separated.
- Audience language and objection formats have evidence guardrails.
- Format selection rules are documented.
- Platform adaptation remains reserved for Phase 5.
- The architecture is ready for the next Phase 3 task: content-to-funnel mapping.
