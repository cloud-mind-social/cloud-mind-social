## 1. Platform roles and operating priorities

The approved platform scope is LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and Threads. X remains excluded. The table records approved working roles; it does not claim tested acquisition performance or require equal production across all channels.

| Platform | Operating role | Intended movement |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Business credibility and relevant professional conversations | Qualified interest and website review requests |
| Facebook | Relationship building, education and community trust | Conversations and appropriate website entry |
| Instagram | Visual recognition and accessible explanations | Profile exploration, method content and website visits |
| YouTube | Deeper explanations and visible methodology | Stronger understanding and diagnostic consideration |
| TikTok | Human presence and problem recognition | Continued attention and exploration |
| Threads | Public thinking and conversation | Relationship development and relevant referral traffic |

Preserve the primary commercial audience: Western growth-stage, owner-led businesses that have outgrown DIY or fragmented social marketing. Western early-stage owner-operators remain a secondary development audience; Philippine businesses remain a separate secondary geographic audience.

Platform role, commercial priority and production capacity are separate decisions. This table does not silently replace the Phase 2.5 acquisition-test priorities. Detailed formats, platform-specific publishing rules and formal platform strategies remain for later implementation.

## 2. Profile and conversion readiness

Before an account becomes active, confirm:

* Approved logo or symbol and visual identity are applied.
* The description identifies who CMS serves and what it does.
* Tagline and methodology language agree with the approved message system.
* Pinned orientation content answers who CMS is, what it believes and why to follow.
* The primary website link continues the profile's message and requested commitment.
* The promoted destination works and has a response owner.
* Comments and inquiries have assigned coverage.
* Commercial claims, capability statements and proof labels pass Phase 3.6 review.

The approved tagline is “We build the bridge from unknown to impact.” This does not finalize every profile bio or public positioning variant.

Do not promote a diagnostic as available before its route is operational. A destination marked pending is suitable for planning, not for a live CTA. Any manual alternative must be approved, tested and communicated honestly.

## 3. Capacity-based planning

Prioritize channels CMS can maintain reliably; expand when capacity and useful audience response justify it. There is no fixed posting quota or requirement to launch all six channels simultaneously.

For each planning cycle, record:

* Available production, design, review and publishing hours.
* Time reserved for community responses and lead handling.
* Original content versus adaptations.
* Active channels and their maintenance commitments.
* Client-delivery demands and specialist availability.
* Work that will be reduced or deferred if capacity tightens.

Do not count the same available hours twice when one person holds multiple roles. Protect lead follow-up and correction work when new-content output is reduced.

Choose a sustainable publishing commitment only after reviewing available capacity. If the plan cannot be maintained, reduce new production or channel scope and record the decision rather than quietly abandoning response commitments.

## 4. Content planning and production

Use Phase 3.8's minimum brief, production statuses and approval gates, including its writing-standard fields and Gate 4 review, as the shared workflow.

### Idea intake and selection

Capture the Phase 3.8 source, voice, permission, attribution and asset-classification fields before selection. Educational content is covered. Hold ideas lacking support or a responsible reviewer rather than filling gaps with invented experiences.

Ideas enter the designated backlog from documented observations, experiences, permissioned conversations, genuine questions, beliefs or reviewed research. Identify an accountable human and distinguish interpretation from firsthand experience. Approved strategic themes route relevance; they cannot substitute for source material. A trend alone does not justify production.

Before selecting an item, identify:

* Audience need and desired realization.
* Pillar, theme, angle and funnel purpose.
* Evidence source, claim limitation and proof type.
* Appropriate format and approved visual template family.
* CTA, verified or staged destination and intended next action.
* Creator, reviewer, due date and effort estimate.

Review pillar and funnel coverage for meaningful gaps; do not impose equal pillar percentages without a strategic reason. Prioritize relevance, evidence, useful next steps and available capacity over posting volume.

### Calendar and release control

The operating calendar should link to the approved brief and record owner, draft due date, review due date, intended release, destination, status and any dependencies. It is a decision record, not only a list of captions and dates.

Approval for adaptation is not publication approval. The final platform version must preserve the approved claim, disclosure, CTA and visual-proof label, and receive the required release authorization.

Before scheduling, verify the destination, offer availability, attribution and permissions. If a dependency fails, hold or revise the item; do not publish a broken route simply to meet a calendar date.

### Correction and repurposing record

Record source/voice context, permission scope and grounding, accountability and expression review for each adapted version. Rebuild pacing for the new context without imposing repeated public structures; changed use requires a permission check and version-specific factual/voice approval.

For a correction, record the affected content, issue, owner, decision, affected channels and follow-up. Correct misleading claims or nonworking destinations promptly within the authorized workflow.

For repurposing, retain a reference to the approved source item, evidence, limitations, intended realization and adaptation approval. A format change must not turn a hypothesis into a result or a method demonstration into client proof.

## 5. Community management and inquiry handling

| Interaction | Response path | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| General question | Provide a useful answer or relevant resource | Do not treat every question as buying intent |
| Relevant business problem | Clarify briefly and offer an appropriate entry route | Do not perform an unpaid full diagnosis in public comments |
| Service inquiry | Capture essential context and hand to the commercial owner | Do not quote unapproved pricing or promise unsupported scope |
| Complaint or evidence challenge | Acknowledge, preserve context and escalate to the accountable owner | Do not argue, invent evidence or disclose client details |
| Spam or inappropriate message | Apply the approved moderation process | Do not confuse legitimate criticism with spam |
| Question needing specialist judgment | Refer to the strategy or commercial owner | State limits rather than improvising an answer |

Move sensitive business context into an appropriate private conversation or website intake route. Do not request account passwords or confidential business records in public comments.

Set response windows based on actual coverage. Do not promise constant availability. If a public response commitment cannot be met, escalate the capacity issue and adjust commitments with approval.

## 6. Social-to-website lead handoff

Use the website routes and qualification controls defined in Phase 3.8.

| Social signal | Appropriate next step |
|---|---|
| Recognizes a problem but cannot explain it | Social Signal Check |
| Describes a specific situation | Signal-to-Strategy Review |
| Expresses readiness for paid diagnosis | Social Clarity Intensive qualification |
| Requests a clearly defined asset | À La Carte scope assessment |
| Engages without buying intent | Continue useful conversation; do not force a sales route |

The ladder is not mandatory. Route according to fit and need. Preserve the distinction between a preliminary check, bounded review and paid diagnostic.

Record source platform, relevant content or conversation, supplied business context, permission preferences, assigned owner, next action and follow-up date. Avoid asking prospects to repeat information already provided.

The commercial owner applies Phase 3.8's fit, problem, urgency and readiness assessment. A like, comment, click or email address is not by itself a qualified opportunity.

Inquiry responses are separate from optional promotional subscriptions. Respect declines and requests to stop follow-up. Do not promise that booking, payment or a lead destination works without verification.

Close the handoff loop by recording received, accepted for follow-up, deferred, declined or another accurately defined outcome. Record reasons where relevant and feed learning back without exposing private prospect information.

## 7. Ownership and continuity

| Function | Accountable responsibility | Backup requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Planning | Select relevant work and balance scope against capacity | Another authorized person can interpret the backlog |
| Writing | Develop a grounded perspective in the approved voice without expanding claims or imposing formulaic structures | Accessible source/voice brief and permissions |
| Design | Apply the approved visual system and proof labels | Approved asset references and editable working material |
| Evidence review | Check grounding, limitations, attribution and permission; distinguish research from lived experience | Escalation to an authorized reviewer |
| Final approval | Record grounding, accountability and expression review, factual/voice confirmation and authorization for the specified version and use | Explicit delegated approver; no assumed approval |
| Publishing | Release only approved versions and verify destinations | Authorized account access and release record |
| Community response | Handle questions and escalate appropriately | Coverage for the declared response window |
| Lead handoff | Transfer context and ensure receipt by the commercial owner | Named alternate for absence or overload |
| Learning | Record signals, review results and propose changes | Shared review record and next actions |

One person may hold several roles; each responsibility still needs a named owner before activation. Owner roles in this document are not completed assignments.

Record absence coverage and the route for recovering or transferring authorized access. Grant only the access needed for the role and remove it when no longer needed. Never store passwords, tokens or recovery codes in this Markdown file.

If coverage cannot be arranged, reduce or pause the affected commitment and communicate the change appropriately. Do not imply a specialist network or continuity guarantee that is not operationally supported.

## 8. Learning and improvement

| Review rhythm | Required review | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Publishing days | Published accuracy, destinations, relevant responses and urgent issues | Corrections, responses and lead handoffs |
| Weekly | Inquiries, follow-up, production bottlenecks, audience questions and upcoming content | Revised priorities, accountable actions and manageable workload |
| Monthly | Channel contribution, capacity, recurring objections and experiments | Continue, revise, expand or reduce with recorded rationale |

Record audience language, question quality, misunderstandings, objections, qualified website movement and commercial follow-through alongside attention metrics. Reach is not a substitute for qualified interest.

Each review should state what was observed, what is inferred, what remains unknown and what should change. Include owner, next action and review date for a proposed change.

Experiments need a hypothesis, bounded scope, owner, observation window and decision rule. Do not interpret a small sample or an engagement spike as proof of market fit. Preserve Phase 3.8 conversion definitions so social and website reporting remain comparable.

## Readiness decision options

Apply Green, Amber or Red to the recorded scope of planning or activation, not to all Phase 3 work by default. A planning approval cannot waive destination testing, named coverage or publication authorization.

### GREEN; Ready for the specified planning scope

Use when:

- Architecture is approved.
- Controls are usable.
- Missing assets do not prevent responsible planning.
- Assumptions are explicit and bounded.

### AMBER; Proceed with controlled assumptions within the specified planning scope

Use when:

- Some proof or destinations remain incomplete.
- The work can proceed using proof of method and qualified language.
- Manual routing or staged destinations are acceptable.
- No unsupported public claim is required.

### RED; Hold the affected planning or activation scope

Use when:

- Pillars conflict with the locked strategy.
- CTA routes depend on unapproved offers.
- Public proof cannot be substantiated.
- The content system cannot be used consistently.
- Audience or commercial direction has become materially unclear.
