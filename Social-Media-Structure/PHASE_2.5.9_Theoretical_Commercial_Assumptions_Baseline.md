# Phase 2.5.9 — Theoretical Commercial Assumptions Baseline

**Status:** Theoretical working draft; research-supported hypotheses; live buyer evidence unavailable  
**Track:** Brand & Strategy; Commercial Viability & Validation Gate  
**Date:** September 17, 2026  
**Phase boundary:** This document defines what CMS can responsibly assume from its current research. It does not claim market validation, willingness to pay, conversion, or product-market fit.

## Purpose

CMS has not yet had sufficient opportunity to speak with prospects. Therefore, this phase cannot be treated as a completed commercial validation.

The current objective is to convert available evidence into an explicit, testable assumptions model.

The central question is:

> What can CMS reasonably believe based on research, and what still requires direct buyer behavior?

This prevents CMS from mistaking strategic confidence, competitor behavior, likes, compliments, or free-resource interest for commercial proof.

## Confidence labels

Each assumption should use one of four labels:

### Research-supported

The assumption is consistent with approved strategy and repeated research patterns. It still needs live buyer evidence.

### Strategic inference

The assumption is a reasonable interpretation of the research, but it was not directly stated or tested by buyers.

### Validation required

The assumption may be correct, but no responsible conclusion can be made without behavioral or commercial evidence.

### Contradicted

New evidence directly conflicts with the assumption and requires revision.

No assumption should be labelled validated before CMS observes relevant buyer behavior.

## What CMS can do before prospect conversations

### 1. Build the baseline model

Map:

> Audience → problem → trigger → hook → diagnostic → offer → price → delivery → follow-on

Mark each connection as research-supported, inferred, or unknown.

### 2. Run internal roleplay

Simulate:

- a Western founder with fragmented social media;
- an early-stage owner needing strategic foundation;
- a prospect comparing CMS with a freelancer;
- a prospect requesting only À La Carte work;
- a prospect questioning price or proof.

This tests clarity and conversation flow. It does not prove demand.

### 3. Run comprehension tests

Show the positioning, hook, and offer to people who resemble the target audience.

Ask:

- Who do you think this is for?
- What problem is being addressed?
- What would you expect to receive?
- What feels unclear?
- What would make you hesitate?
- What would you expect to happen next?

This tests understanding, not willingness to buy.

### 4. Run low-risk public behavior tests

CMS can observe:

- Social Signal Check visits and completions;
- saves and shares of diagnosis-based content;
- replies to problem-focused questions;
- profile-to-website clicks;
- requests for examples, pricing, or next steps;
- responses to permission-based invitations.

These are early signals only. They are not revenue evidence.

### 5. Prepare the live validation protocol

Before outreach begins, define:

- who to contact;
- what to ask;
- what counts as qualified interest;
- what counts as a buying signal;
- what price or offer to present;
- what evidence would weaken the assumption;
- what decision follows each result.

## Evidence that will eventually be needed

Theoretical assumptions should later be tested through:

- completed qualification forms;
- attended discovery conversations;
- detailed prospect replies;
- requests for scope or proposals;
- access to relevant business information;
- accepted paid diagnostics;
- deposits or signed engagements;
- repeat requests;
- referrals from relevant business owners or partners;
- measured delivery effort and margin.

The strongest evidence is not enthusiasm. It is a meaningful commitment of money, time, information, access, or reputation.

## Commercial evidence classification

| Observed behavior | Evidence strength | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Paid diagnostic, deposit, or signed engagement | High | Direct commercial evidence |
| Prospect provides data, access, or introduces a decision-maker | Medium to high | Serious commitment signal |
| Prospect attends a qualified discovery conversation | Medium | Stronger than casual interest |
| Prospect requests proposal, price, or scope detail | Medium | Commercial curiosity; not yet purchase proof |
| Prospect completes the Social Signal Check | Low to medium | Recognition signal |
| Prospect likes, compliments, or downloads a free resource | Low | Awareness or social approval only |
| Prospect says “this is a great idea” without action | Very low | Do not treat as validation |

- No theoretical conclusion is presented as validated demand.
