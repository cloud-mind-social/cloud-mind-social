## Purpose

Phase 2.5.10 is the decision gate between Commercial Architecture and formal Phase 3 Content Architecture.

CMS is still in its initial testing stage. Sufficient prospect conversations and paid buyer evidence are not yet available. The responsible operating position is therefore to proceed with explicit assumptions based on:

- approved Phase 1 strategic decisions;
- approved Phase 2 Part 1 and Part 2 audience architecture;
- approved Phase 2.5 decisions and working drafts;
- completed Track B audience research;
- Track A research findings approved for use as an evidence input;
- agency and freelancer competitor observations;
- CMS's documented strategic interpretation of the evidence.

The assumptions must remain visible, testable, and revisable as behavioral evidence becomes available.

## Decision

**Decision outcome: Proceed with explicit assumptions.**

CMS may continue controlled preparation toward Phase 3 using the current research base. Every theoretical conclusion, provisional figure, or untested buyer-response assumption must be labeled clearly.

This decision does not remove the need for validation. It changes the operating posture from “wait for perfect evidence” to “move forward with documented assumptions and defined correction points.”

## Assumption operating model

Each input used in Phase 3 preparation must be classified as one of the following:

| Label | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| Approved decision | Explicitly accepted strategic direction | May guide strategy and messaging |
| Research-supported assumption | Consistent with the approved evidence base but not confirmed through CMS buyer behavior | May guide testing and provisional content |
| Strategic inference | CMS interpretation of several findings | May guide exploration; must not be presented as fact |
| Validation required | Cannot be responsibly resolved without live behavioral or commercial evidence | Must remain on the validation backlog |
| Contradicted | New evidence conflicts with the current assumption | Requires review and possible revision |

No assumption becomes validated merely because it is repeated, receives praise, earns likes, or appears reasonable.
