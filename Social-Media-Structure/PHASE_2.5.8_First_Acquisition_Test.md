# Phase 2.5.8 — First Acquisition Test

**Status:** Working draft; first test not yet run  
**Track:** Brand & Strategy; Commercial Viability & Validation Gate  
**Date:** September 17, 2026  
**Phase boundary:** This document designs the first acquisition experiment. It does not authorize a full platform launch, paid advertising, mass outbound, or permanent sales policy.

## Purpose

Test whether CMS can generate qualified sales conversations from the approved commercial audience before investing in broad content production, paid advertising, or full platform activation.

The objective is learning and commercial evidence:

- which prospects respond;
- which problem language creates curiosity;
- which hook offer attracts qualified attention;
- which channel produces useful conversations;
- which objections block progression;
- whether qualified prospects will pay for diagnosis;
- whether early-stage relationships create future referrals or partnerships.

## Core acquisition hypothesis

### Primary hypothesis

Western founder or owner-led expertise and high-value service businesses with fragmented or unclear social media activity will respond more strongly to a diagnosis-first invitation than to a generic social media management pitch.

### Secondary hypothesis

Western early-stage owner-operators may not be immediately ready for a larger engagement, but a bounded foundation conversation can create future clients, referrals, and strategic partnerships.

### Channel hypothesis

A relationship-led route using warm introductions, permission-based outreach, LinkedIn, Facebook, and the CMS website will create more useful learning at this stage than paid ads or mass cold outreach.

### Offer hypothesis

The Social Signal Check should create curiosity. The Signal-to-Strategy Review should qualify interest. The Social Clarity Intensive should test willingness to pay for diagnosis.

## Recommended acquisition route

Use one acquisition loop with three source lanes:

1. Warm introductions and referrals.
2. Permission-based direct outreach.
3. Relationship-building with potential connectors.

Use:

- LinkedIn as the primary prospecting channel for Western professional and B2B audiences.
- Facebook as a secondary relationship and community channel for owner-led businesses.
- The website as the owned conversion and qualification environment.
- Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and Threads as credibility and nurture channels during the test.

Do not launch paid advertising yet. Do not send mass unsolicited messages. CMS is still validating positioning, proof, pricing, and offer fit; paid traffic would amplify unresolved weaknesses.

## Acquisition lanes

| Lane | Audience | Immediate goal | Commercial treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary commercial lane | Western founder or owner-led expertise and high-value service businesses | Generate qualified diagnostic opportunities | Personalized outreach, referrals, discovery, Social Clarity Intensive |
| Secondary development lane | Western early-stage owner-operators | Build relationships, referrals, partnerships, and future pipeline | Education, Social Signal Check, selective conversations |
| Connector lane | Designers, developers, consultants, fractional CMOs, coaches, and adjacent specialists | Generate trusted introductions | Partnership conversations and referral exploration |

Early-stage leads should not be judged only by immediate revenue. Their value may be future conversion, a referral, a collaboration, or better market intelligence.

## First test funnel

### ToFu: Social Signal Check

Purpose:

- Create a useful business question.
- Help the prospect recognize a possible social or positioning problem.
- Start a permission-based conversation.
- Avoid selling management before the problem is understood.

Working invitation:

> “I’m testing a short Social Signal Check for founder-led service businesses. It helps identify whether a social presence is supporting discovery and trust, or only keeping accounts active. Would you like to see it?”

Possible formats:

- Short self-assessment.
- Platform profile checklist.
- Brief personalized observation.
- Educational post leading to the check.
- Warm referral introduction.

### MoFu: Signal-to-Strategy Review

Invite prospects who:

- complete or discuss the Social Signal Check;
- have a recognizable business problem;
- influence or own the decision;
- have a relevant timing trigger;
- are open to strategic discussion.

The review should clarify the likely problem, the business consequence, and whether further diagnosis is justified.

### BoFu: Social Clarity Intensive

Offer the paid diagnostic when the prospect has:

- a defined problem;
- a meaningful business consequence;
- a realistic need for outside help;
- willingness to invest in diagnosis;
- a clear next decision point.

The diagnostic should end with one of three recommendations:

1. A scoped CMS engagement.
2. A future timing or internal action.
3. No CMS engagement because the prospect is not a fit.

The diagnostic must remain finite. It must not silently become unpaid strategy development.

## Initial test cohort

The first test may begin with:

- 15 carefully selected primary prospects.
- 5 early-stage owner-operators.
- 5 potential referral or partnership connectors.

Use one consistent core invitation, with small language variations by audience lane.

The exact number may be adjusted to Seul's capacity and the finalized Phase 2.5.6 pricing.

## Prospect selection criteria

### Primary prospect fit

Prioritize prospects who show several of these conditions:

- Founder or owner is visible in the business.
- Offer requires trust, expertise, or a considered buying decision.
- Business serves a Western market or has Western expansion intent.
- Social presence exists but feels fragmented, inconsistent, unclear, or underused.
- Owner or team is burdened by content decisions.
- Business has a meaningful offer and some traction.
- A current trigger exists; launch, repositioning, expansion, team change, or stalled growth.
- Decision-maker access is possible.
- A paid diagnostic could reasonably fit the business context.

### Secondary prospect fit

Prioritize early-stage owner-operators who have:

- a real offer or credible direction;
- early traction or a clear launch path;
- willingness to clarify the business before producing content;
- a defined foundation need;
- potential to become a future client, referrer, or partner.

Do not accept undefined “pick my brain” requests without a bounded scope.

## Outreach message structure

Every message should contain:

1. Relevant context.
2. A specific observation or reason for contact.
3. A business question.
4. A low-pressure invitation.
5. A clear permission step.

Avoid:

- generic “we help businesses grow” openings;
- unsolicited full audits;
- long service menus;
- pressure to book immediately;
- unsupported results;
- fake scarcity;
- claims that CMS has worked with brands it has not served.

## Qualification questions

Record:

- business type;
- location and market;
- business stage;
- primary offer;
- current social channels;
- current social objective;
- perceived problem;
- business consequence;
- what has already been tried;
- why the conversation is happening now;
- decision-maker and stakeholders;
- expected timeline;
- desired support; strategy, creation, scheduling, management, or growth partnership;
- readiness for a paid diagnostic;
- potential referral or partnership value.

## Lead temperature and routing

| Signal | Lead temperature | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| Asks about pricing, scope, availability, or timing | Hot | Move toward discovery or Social Clarity Intensive |
| Completes the Social Signal Check and discusses a real problem | Warm to hot | Offer Signal-to-Strategy Review |
| Engages with educational content or accepts a resource | Warm | Continue conversation and qualify |
| Comes through a trusted referral | Warm to hot | Respond personally and route to the appropriate next step |
| Shows no meaningful engagement or clear problem | Cold | Nurture without a hard pitch |
| Does not need CMS but has relevant network access | Connector | Explore referral or partnership relationship |
| Requests unlimited coaching or undefined support | Unqualified | Re-scope before continuing |

## Discovery conversation

The discovery conversation should answer:

1. What is the business trying to make easier, clearer, or more credible?
2. What does the owner believe social media should accomplish?
3. What is currently happening instead?
4. What has the problem cost in time, confidence, opportunities, or revenue?
5. What changed recently?
6. What would a useful improvement look like?
7. What has already been attempted?
8. What kind of support would be acceptable?
9. What investment and timeline are realistic?
10. What should CMS recommend against?

The call is for diagnosis and fit. It is not a free content planning session.

## Follow-up workflow

### Within one business day

Send:

- concise summary of the stated problem;
- distinction between symptom and likely underlying constraint;
- key question still unresolved;
- recommended next step;
- clear statement if CMS is not the right fit.

### If the prospect is qualified

Offer:

- Signal-to-Strategy Review;
- Social Clarity Intensive;
- an appropriate À La Carte service only when the problem is already clear;
- Presence, Momentum, Authority, or Growth Partner only when the depth of responsibility justifies it.

### If the prospect is not ready

Send:

- one relevant educational resource;
- a future timing suggestion;
- permission to stay in touch;
- a note about what would make the conversation timely.

### If the prospect is a connector

Record:

- audience served;
- type of relationship;
- overlap with CMS ICP;
- possible referral situation;
- agreed next action;
- no automatic commission or referral promise until separately approved.

## Ownership

The test requires explicit ownership of:

- prospect list;
- outreach;
- response handling;
- qualification;
- discovery;
- diagnosis synthesis;
- proposal or diagnostic offer;
- follow-up;
- tracking;
- weekly review.

The person leading the conversation must be able to explain CMS's diagnosis-first approach. Technical, analytics, or specialist support should be added only when relevant to the prospect's diagnosed need.

## Measurement framework

Track each contact:

- source lane;
- platform;
- audience layer;
- message version;
- hook used;
- date contacted;
- response;
- response quality;
- qualification status;
- discovery status;
- offer presented;
- objection;
- next step;
- paid diagnostic decision;
- follow-on fit;
- referral or partnership potential;
- time spent.

Do not use reach or follower growth as the primary success measure for this experiment.

## Provisional success criteria

The first test should be considered useful if it produces:

- at least 5 substantive conversations;
- at least 3 commercially qualified primary prospects;
- at least 1 paid diagnostic opportunity or a clear pricing objection;
- at least 2 useful referral or partnership conversations;
- repeated buyer language that confirms or challenges the current positioning;
- clear evidence about which hook and channel generated the strongest response.

A non-conversion is still useful when the reason is documented; lack of urgency, unclear offer, weak proof, wrong audience, budget mismatch, poor timing, or lack of decision authority.

## Decision gate

### Pass

Continue the route when:

- qualified prospects recognize the problem;
- the invitation creates meaningful responses;
- the diagnostic is understood;
- at least one paid opportunity or strong purchase signal appears;
- the delivery path is manageable.

### Revise

Change the message, hook, qualification, or offer when:

- people respond but do not understand the category;
- conversations are positive but commercially vague;
- prospects want execution but not diagnosis;
- the primary and secondary lanes are being mixed;
- price objections indicate unclear scope or value.

### Stop or pause

Pause the route when:

- responses are consistently unqualified;
- the audience does not recognize the problem;
- the offer creates excessive unpaid work;
- the channel requires unsustainable effort;
- evidence contradicts the ICP or positioning.

## Guardrails

- No mass unsolicited direct messaging.
- No paid ads before the offer and proof are validated.
- No free custom strategy for every respondent.
- No guaranteed leads, revenue, or growth.
- No fabricated case studies, testimonials, logos, or results.
- No automatic discounting.
- No referral commission promise without approval.
- No permanent pricing decision from one conversation.
- No conversion of early-stage relationship work into unlimited unpaid support.
- No full six-platform launch during this test.
