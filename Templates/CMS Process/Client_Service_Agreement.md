## 2. Agreed services and documents

CMS will perform the services expressly included in **Schedule A**, which forms part of this agreement. A website description, discovery conversation, or draft recommendation does not add services beyond that schedule.

The selected path is [Content & Execution / Managed Presence / Strategy & Growth / Strategic Partnership / separately described finite service]. Deliverables, platforms, quantities, management responsibilities, exclusions, reporting, and deadlines must be stated in Schedule A.

In case of inconsistency, a later amendment accepted by both parties takes priority. An expressly identified exception in Schedule A takes priority over the corresponding general commercial term. This agreement takes priority over an attached proposal unless the schedule expressly incorporates a specific proposal provision. No document overrides applicable mandatory law.

## 3. Start conditions and service process

The parties will confirm the work sequence in Schedule A. Where strategy and production are included, the normal sequence is agreement and required initial payment; intake and assets; review and strategy; presentation and approval; production; content review and approval; release or scheduling; execution; reporting; handover or renewal.

CMS starts the applicable stage after the payment due for that stage, required information, and agreed access are available. Production that depends on strategy starts only after the relevant strategy and calendar are approved and required assets are complete. Schedule A may permit production before a final balance that becomes due at a later milestone.

A preparation period and a publishing period are separate. The schedule identifies whether an ongoing publishing cycle starts on the first scheduled post, a fixed date, or another agreed event. A discovery call does not guarantee immediate production or reserve an unconfirmed slot.

## 4. CMS responsibilities

CMS will use reasonable care and skill, follow the agreed scope and approved direction, identify the delivery contact, and communicate material issues affecting the work. CMS will keep approval and delivery records and provide the reports and handover specified in Schedule A.

CMS may involve the specialists named or authorized in Schedule A. Anyone receiving confidential information must be bound by suitable confidentiality obligations. CMS remains accountable for its contracted services. A material change to a named specialist will be communicated; it does not authorize a change in scope or price without agreement.

CMS will not publish unapproved content, incur advertising spend beyond the agreed written authorization, or commit the Client to a purchase or third-party agreement without authority. If community management is included, the schedule defines what CMS may answer and what must be escalated.

## 5. Client responsibilities and access

The Client supplies accurate business details, approved offers, brand references, assets, source information, and access needed for the agreed services. The Client confirms that it has the necessary rights and permissions for material it supplies, including relevant customer images or testimonials.

The Client designates one authorized person to consolidate feedback and approvals. The Client remains responsible for its products, prices, availability, business claims, customer service, sales decisions, and fulfillment. CMS's review does not independently verify a claim unless that verification is expressly included.

The Client retains ownership and control of its accounts. Access will use appropriate role-based permissions where available; passwords and recovery control should remain with the Client. CMS may use access only for the agreed work. Removing required access during the engagement may delay or prevent delivery; CMS will explain the effect and the parties will agree the next step.

At expiry or termination, CMS will stop using account access when its agreed duties end and assist with removal of its permissions. The parties will specify any access needed for final reporting or handover. Accounts are not collateral for unpaid fees.

## 6. Fees, payment allocation, and external costs

The Client pays the currency, amounts, milestones, and due dates in Schedule A. The schedule identifies taxes, invoice requirements, payment method, transfer charges, and any authorized external costs. Advertising spend, subscriptions, stock licences, travel, and outside production are included only if expressly stated.

Payments will be credited to their named milestone or invoice. A payment allocated to delivered review, strategy, or calendar work does not also pay a separate production balance. CMS will confirm payments received and outstanding amounts.

Fees for completed work delivered in accordance with the agreement remain payable if the Client changes its mind. This is not a blanket forfeiture of payment for undelivered services. Settlement of a cancellation or service failure follows Section 14 and applicable law.

Clean final files, scheduling, or another release milestone may be conditional on the payment expressly identified in Schedule A. The applicable stage is not withheld for a future amount that is not yet due. No additional charge, late fee, or automatic renewal is implied by this template.

## 7. Approvals and revision limits

CMS submits the item or batch with an identifiable version and a deadline through the agreed channel. The Client approves it or provides consolidated feedback within the response window in Schedule A. Silence is not approval.

Included revision rounds, the unit they apply to, and the permitted changes are stated in Schedule A. A revision adjusts work within the approved direction and scope. A new concept, additional deliverable, replacement asset requiring rework, or additional round may require a change order.

CMS will correct its errors or work that fails the agreed specification without treating those corrections as an extra creative revision. Client approval does not excuse CMS from its own breach or remove rights that cannot lawfully be waived. Changes made by the Client or another party after approval may require fresh review and a separate scope.

## 8. Delays, pauses, and production slots

Missing assets, approvals, access, or payments currently due may shift dependent deadlines. CMS will communicate the reason and revised timing. If Schedule A expressly permits slot release after a missed deadline, CMS may pause and release the slot after notifying the Client.

Resumed work enters the next available agreed slot once the missing requirements are met; original dates are not automatically retained. Silence or slot release does not automatically terminate this agreement.

If CMS causes a material delay, CMS will inform the Client and propose a revised delivery or appropriate remedy. A pause does not create extra charges without written agreement or remove obligations for undelivered work. A prolonged pause is handled under the cancellation or termination provisions.

## 9. Changes and additional work

A change request must state the added or changed work, fee, effect on timing, and responsible approvers. CMS performs that work only after both parties accept the change in writing. An informal request does not create unlimited support. A signed amendment is required for a material change to legal terms.

## 10. Content rights, licences, and handover

The Client retains rights to Client-supplied materials. After payment of the amount allocated to a final deliverable, CMS assigns to the Client its transferable copyright and ownership in the original final content expressly created for the Client under this agreement, subject to the following exceptions.

CMS retains its pre-existing templates, reusable systems, methods, tools, and generic components. If these are embedded in a final deliverable, CMS grants the Client a nonexclusive, perpetual licence to use them as part of that deliverable for its business, including modification of the delivered work. Separate redistribution or resale of CMS's reusable template library is not included.

Third-party fonts, music, stock, software, and platform assets remain subject to their licences. CMS will identify material restrictions known to it; it cannot assign rights it does not hold. AI-assisted work must be reviewed for the agreed purpose; CMS does not promise exclusive rights in material where such rights are unavailable. CMS will obtain from its contributors the rights it needs to make the agreed assignment.

Unused concepts, drafts, working files, source code, and editable design files are included only if specified in Schedule A. The agreed final file types, captions, report, delivery route, recipient, and editable-file inclusions are recorded there. CMS may provide a ZIP attachment where selected; a different route or split archive requires agreement if attachment limits prevent delivery.

## 11. Portfolio and case-study permission

The portfolio selection in Schedule A controls whether CMS may showcase the paid final work and public business identity. If permission is granted, CMS may use only the approved categories and must observe any embargo or approval condition recorded there.

Private messages, customer information, credentials, confidential business data, and nonpublic analytics are excluded from portfolio permission. Publishing these, a testimonial, or a claim about the Client's results requires separate written authorization and appropriate evidence. Confidentiality continues regardless of the portfolio choice. If no choice is made, permission is not granted.

## 12. Confidentiality and personal information

Each party will use the other's nonpublic information only for this engagement, restrict access to people who need it, and apply reasonable safeguards. Confidential information excludes information already lawfully public, independently developed, or lawfully obtained without a confidentiality duty. A legally required disclosure will be limited to what is required, with notice where lawful.

Where CMS handles leads, inbox messages, or other personal information for the Client, processing must follow documented instructions and applicable privacy law. Before such access begins, Schedule A or a separate data-processing addendum must identify the purpose, information categories, permitted tools and recipients, processing locations, access limits, incident contacts, and return or deletion arrangements. CMS will not put private customer data into AI tools unless that use is authorized and appropriate safeguards are in place.

The parties will promptly notify each other of a relevant suspected security incident, cooperate on containment and assessment, and meet applicable notification duties. Contractual notification timing must not delay a legal deadline. CMS will return or delete Client information as agreed, except records it must lawfully retain; retained records remain protected.

## 13. Platforms, advertising, and outcomes

CMS commits to the agreed services and professional care. It does not guarantee sales, revenue, follower growth, reach, virality, advertising approval, or a particular platform result. Progress is assessed against the agreed objective and measures with their limitations.

Third-party platforms control their systems, algorithms, policies, and enforcement. CMS will inform the Client of material platform restrictions affecting the work and take reasonable steps within the agreed scope. This clause does not excuse CMS errors, unauthorized activity, or failure to follow the agreement.

Paid advertising requires separate scope, spending authority, account arrangements, and reporting. Creating an ad-style visual does not itself include campaign setup, media buying, or management.

## 14. Termination, cancellation, and settlement

The engagement ends on the date or completion event in Schedule A unless both parties agree a renewal. Renewal requires a confirmed scope and price; it is not automatic.

Either party may terminate for a material breach after written notice identifying the issue and the cure period in Schedule A, if the breach remains uncured. A party may suspend an affected activity immediately when reasonably necessary to prevent unlawful conduct or a serious security risk, explaining the reason as soon as practicable. Convenience cancellation follows the notice arrangement in Schedule A.

On termination, CMS will provide a written account of delivered work, reasonable documented work in progress within the authorized scope, approved nonrecoverable external costs, payments received, and any balance or refund. CMS will not automatically charge the whole remaining contract price for unperformed future services. Undisputed amounts for completed conforming work remain due. Any unused advance remaining after amounts properly payable are deducted will be returned within the settlement period in Schedule A, subject to applicable law.

The Client receives the paid final deliverables and agreed reporting or handover. The parties will arrange account-access removal and address any live or scheduled campaigns. No clause removes remedies for CMS nonperformance, fraud, or rights that cannot lawfully be excluded.

## 15. Liability, disruptions, and disputes

Each party remains responsible for its own breach, negligent acts, and unauthorized conduct under applicable law. Any additional liability limitation must be specifically set out in a signed schedule or amendment and reviewed for the governing jurisdiction. This master contains no automatic liability cap or blanket indemnity. No limitation excuses fraud, willful misconduct, or liability that cannot lawfully be limited.

An event beyond a party's reasonable control that prevents performance must be communicated promptly. The parties will mitigate its effects and agree a revised timetable or termination and settlement if performance cannot reasonably resume. Completed obligations and payments already due remain subject to this agreement.

The parties will first seek a good-faith resolution through their authorized contacts. Schedule A identifies the agreed escalation period, governing law, and dispute forum. Applicable mandatory rights and procedures remain in force. Arbitration applies only if the parties enter an express, reviewed arbitration agreement.

## 16. Notices, entire agreement, and signing

Formal notices go to the addresses in Schedule A. Routine approvals may use the separately identified approval channel. Changes to contact details must be communicated in writing.

This agreement and its completed schedules contain the agreed terms and replace prior discussions about the same engagement. If a provision is unenforceable, the remaining provisions continue to the extent lawful; neither party gains a right to rewrite the bargain unilaterally.

The parties may sign counterparts or use an agreed electronic signing method that supports identity, authority, consent, integrity, and a retained signed record, subject to applicable formalities. Neither a discovery meeting nor an unsigned blank template constitutes the executed agreement.
