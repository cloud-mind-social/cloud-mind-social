## 3. What happens next
### Step 1 ; intake and readiness review
You verify the intake, supply available source material, and identify the approver. CMS reviews the information, checks file access, and sends one consolidated list of remaining requirements. If something will affect delivery, we agree on the next action and revised timing under the contract.

### Step 2 ; briefing or kickoff
We confirm the objective, deliverables, audience, voice, approval route, dependencies and first milestone. A meeting is held only if included or agreed; a written briefing can serve the same purpose for smaller engagements.

### Step 3 ; work and first review
CMS prepares the contracted work and presents the exact files or controlled links for review. Strategic direction or a calendar is presented first where it is part of the scope. The review request identifies the item/version, decision needed and deadline.

### Step 4 ; feedback, approval and release
The named approver returns consolidated feedback. CMS applies included revisions and resolves its own errors as the agreement provides. Final delivery or publishing follows only after the required approvals and payment milestone are met. Scheduling requires express inclusion and release permission.

### Step 5 ; handover or ongoing cycle
You receive the agreed final files and usage instructions. Managed engagements continue through the agreed publishing, reporting and review cycle. Project engagements close with the contracted handover and any agreed support period.

### How this changes by service depth
Content & Execution: confirm the brief, formats, quantities, supplied direction and assets; review content; receive the contracted deliverables. Publishing, analytics and ongoing management apply only if expressly included.
Managed Presence: confirm accounts, monthly calendar, operational access, content approvals, publishing responsibility and reporting cadence. Community or inbox work requires a defined scope, escalation route and coverage.
Strategy & Growth: confirm the problem, available evidence, stakeholder input and workshop needs; review recommendations; receive the strategy and implementation handoff. Implementation, production and paid-media operation require separate inclusion.
Strategic Partnership: confirm decision rights, stakeholder roles, planning cadence, dependencies, reporting access and the agreed implementation responsibilities. A retainer does not imply unlimited tasks or continuous availability.

## 4. Information, assets and access
### Required for the first brief
- Correct business and public contact details; current products/services, offer terms, prices and availability where the work mentions them.
- Audience, location, language, priority goal and agreed success measures.
- Brand name, approved logos, colors/fonts if available, voice guidance and examples.
- Source facts for claims, testimonials, credentials and case studies; identify the owner who can verify them.
- Main contact, authorized approver, restrictions, important dates and current asset links.

### Required only when the scope needs them
- Publishing or management: exact platform/account links, role-based access, calendar timezone, scheduling owner and escalation contacts.
- Measurement or strategy: relevant baseline reports, date range, definitions and existing research; aggregated or redacted data where sufficient.
- Campaign or lead-flow work: destination links, current offer, lead-handling owner, capacity, tracking and ad budget if expressly included.
- Website or technical work: relevant hosting, domain or analytics access through the agreed secure method.
- Editable production: licensed brand fonts, templates or project files where needed and rights permit use.

Request only data needed for the engagement. Mark unavailable information as unknown; do not guess or invent proof.

### Asset submission
Upload originals to 02_Assets. Include approved logo variants, useful photos/video, product images and relevant prior materials. Preserve source quality; avoid screenshots of images when the originals are available. Include a short note explaining subject, purpose, restrictions and where each asset came from.
Use a consistent name such as [Client]_[Asset description]_[Version].[extension]. CMS assigns content IDs and versions for production and approval. Keep original source files separate from final approved outputs.
Identify whether CMS may edit and publish each supplied asset, where permission is limited, and whether recognizable people, music, stock, fonts or third-party marks need additional clearance. Customer stories and private messages need explicit publication permission.
Mark references as inspiration or licensed assets. A link to an example does not establish reuse rights. If a needed asset is missing, CMS will confirm an alternative or scope change before proceeding.

### Account access
Use named platform roles with the minimum permissions needed. The client retains ownership and recovery control. CMS confirms the authorized team identity before any invitation.
Do not place passwords, recovery codes, payment-card details or private customer databases in the intake, shared folder or ordinary messages. Use the agreed secure access route. Confirm the account and role, access owner, grant/revoke dates and two-factor requirements.
Flag confidentiality and personal-data restrictions before sharing. Any use of nonpublic client material in AI or other third-party tools must follow the agreed processing arrangement and client permissions.
When work ends, access removal and file retention follow the agreement; renewal requires separately confirmed arrangements.

## 7. Approval responsibilities
CMS is responsible for presenting the contracted work, identifying decisions needed, checking its work, maintaining version control and recording approvals. The client is responsible for confirming business facts, offer terms, permissions, links and any required internal or specialist sign-off.
Final approver: [name and role]. Authorized backup: [ ]. Approval scope: [content / strategy / publishing / other].
Only the named authorized approver gives the final decision. Other stakeholders send comments through the coordinator. CMS does not infer approval from silence, a missed deadline or unrelated reactions.

A review request specifies:
- Content ID or document title; exact version and controlled link.
- Decision requested and any unresolved factual or rights question.
- Response deadline, including timezone and the contract's business-day definition.
- Proposed publication/delivery timing, responsible person and restrictions.

Approval reply template:
“Approved: [item IDs and exact versions / link]. The business facts, offer details and supplied permissions are confirmed. Release authorization: [CMS may publish/schedule to named account at agreed time / client will publish / no publication authorized]. Restrictions: [ ]. Approver: [name and role].”

Changes reply template:
“Changes requested: [item/version]. Consolidated feedback: [specific changes]. Pending verification or permission: [ ]. Approver/coordinator: [name].”

An approval covers the identified version and stated use. Material changes require a new review. Strategy approval alone does not authorize every future asset. CMS-owned errors and client preference changes follow the agreement's distinct correction and revision rules.
Revision allowance and review deadline: [copy from the executed schedule]. Additional work, new deliverables or changed direction require a confirmed scope/fee/timeline change before CMS proceeds. Do not apply promotional review windows to other engagements automatically.
