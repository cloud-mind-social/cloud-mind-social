**Purpose:** Understand the business, demonstrate relevant judgment, assess mutual fit, and identify the appropriate next decision and possible service path.  
**Suggested duration:** 35–40 minutes; adapt to the prospect and meeting context.  
**Audience:** CMS facilitator; prospect-facing wording is supplied where useful.

## 1. Service naming and use rules

Use the four current website paths: **Content & Execution**, **Managed Presence**, **Strategy & Growth**, and **Strategic Partnership**. The website presents their commercial models respectively as per deliverable, monthly package, scoped project fee, and ongoing retainer. These are service depths, not four posting-volume packages.

The September 17 pricing architecture still names Presence, Momentum, Authority, and Growth Partner and contains provisional price bands. This template does not assert a one-to-one equivalence or approve those figures. Use the website path names in conversation; confirm the actual deliverables, fee, responsibilities, and available capacity separately.

The first call establishes understanding and fit. Keep any service-path hypothesis internal until the relevant business context has been reviewed. Offer a firm recommendation only after that review. When a follow-up review is required, explain what will be reviewed and when the prospect will hear back.

Choose questions that follow the conversation. Do not read every prompt as a questionnaire. An answer to one question may cover several fields. Discovery is a bounded conversation; a full strategy, content calendar, or campaign plan requires separately agreed scope.

Completed records contain private prospect information; keep them in the authorized private workspace. Do not publish the completed template, credentials, meeting recording, or quotations. Ask permission before recording; meeting participation is not permission to use a story in marketing.

## 6. Problem, consequence, and desired outcome; about 7 minutes

**Core questions**

“What made you look for support now?”

“How is the current situation affecting the business or your workload?”

“What would a useful improvement look like, and how would we recognize it?”

**Follow where needed**

- If they want more content: “What would more content help you accomplish?”
- If they want more leads: “What happens after someone messages, submits a form, or asks about your offer?”
- If they want more followers: “Which people do you need to reach, and what should happen after they find you?”
- If they need to delegate: “Which decisions and tasks do you want to hand over; which should remain with you?”
- If urgency is unclear: “Is there a date or business event we should plan around?”
- If the constraint may be elsewhere: “What other parts of the customer journey might affect that outcome?”

## 9. Internal service-path routing; complete after the call

**The following decision rules are approved working facilitator guidance derived from the website's four paths and the repository's diagnosis and qualification principles. They are not an approved numerical scoring model or an automatic offer.**

| Current website path | Strong fit signals to investigate | Questions that resolve the depth | Hold or redirect when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content & Execution | A defined deliverable; usable direction and inputs; the client owns strategy and ongoing management | “Is the direction already decided?” “Who will publish and assess the work?” | The client expects business strategy or ongoing management inside a deliverable fee; inputs or rights are missing |
| Managed Presence | Recurring social activity needs an owner; production, approvals, posting, responses, and reporting need coordination | “What should happen each month without coming back to you?” “Who handles inquiries beyond the agreed community scope?” | The outcome requires a major positioning or conversion diagnosis first; client approval or access cannot support delivery |
| Strategy & Growth | A defined strategic or campaign problem; positioning, audience, measurement, or conversion needs investigation and a scoped intervention | “Which decision needs to become clearer?” “What evidence and implementation resources are available?” | The problem is too broad to scope; paid work is requested without the required expertise, budget, access, or measurement |
| Strategic Partnership | A continuing need for marketing leadership, coordination, and decisions across channels or specialists | “Which decisions need an ongoing external owner?” “What authority and team coordination would be available?” | The founder expects unlimited support; leadership authority or specialist delivery capacity is unavailable |

**Three distinctions to record**

1. **Depth of need:** What level of responsibility would address the business problem?
2. **Commercial readiness:** What can the prospect reasonably authorize and fund now?
3. **Delivery readiness:** What can CMS reliably deliver with confirmed people, time, and dependencies?

Do not reduce a leadership need to a basic posting package just because the budget is lower. Define a useful bounded intervention, defer, or redirect. Conversely, a healthy business with a clear one-off production need does not automatically require a retainer.

**Other valid next steps**

| Condition | Possible next step; confirm availability and scope |
|---|---|
| Underlying cause is unclear | A bounded Signal-to-Strategy Review; free or paid status must be confirmed |
| Active business problem needs deeper diagnosis | Separately scoped Social Clarity Intensive; confirm its current price and availability |
| Early-stage business has a real offer and a defined foundation gap | A finite foundation engagement or appropriate scoped help; confirm the offer is operationally available |
| Only scheduling or another isolated operational task is needed | A bounded one-off service under the appropriate scope |
| Prospect asks about the introductory campaign | Review the current approved campaign document and eligibility; record it separately from the four-depth hypothesis |
| Interested but no current trigger or commitment | Permission-based nurture with one useful resource and an agreed revisit point |
| The main constraint falls outside CMS's remit or capacity | Redirect, refer if appropriate, or decline with a clear reason |

**Suggested explanation**

“Based on [reviewed evidence], the priority is [constraint]. We recommend [scope] through [path, when appropriate] because you need [responsibility]. That would cover [work] and depend on [client inputs]. We would assess progress through [measures], with [limitations]. We can prepare a proposal setting out the fee, timing, responsibilities, and review process.”

**Proof and trust guidance**

- Demonstrate business understanding through accurate reflection and a reasoned scope.
- Explain who does the work and how communication and review will happen once confirmed.
- Share only relevant, verifiable proof with permission; identify illustrative examples clearly.
- If no comparable case exists, explain the proposed method and confirmed capability without suggesting an established result.
- Treat process clarity as useful reassurance; never promise specialist coverage or operational resilience that has not been established.
- Respond to concerns about previous providers by defining responsibilities, dependencies, and review points; avoid disparaging their work.

## 13. Final completeness check

- The prospect's stated goal, actual words, and previous attempts are recorded.
- Facts, self-reported consequences, hypotheses, and unknowns are distinguishable.
- Service depth, purchase readiness, and CMS capacity were assessed separately.
- The website names are used without importing provisional pricing or assuming equivalence with older tiers.
- No guaranteed outcomes, fabricated proof, or unconfirmed capability claims were made.
- The prospect corrected or confirmed the call summary.
- Materials, next action, owner, channel, and deadline are clear.
- The completed record is stored privately and linked to the existing Prospect Conversation Record where used; do not maintain conflicting duplicate facts.
- A separately scoped proposal, diagnostic, or production engagement still requires its own agreement.
