## How to Read This Document

Each finding is tagged with a confidence level based on the source type:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **High** | Named industry body (ANA, 4As) or a multi-year, methodologically-described survey (sample size + method disclosed) |
| 🟡 **Moderate** | Single-year agency/vendor survey or trade-press research with a disclosed sample, but narrower scope or one-time study |
| 🟠 **Anecdotal** | Practitioner commentary, forum threads, or single-source qualitative accounts — directionally useful, not statistically representative |
| 🔴 **Unverified** | A statistic cited secondhand that could not be traced to its original source; included only where flagged, and excluded from any CMS strategic claim |

CMS's audience is **not** enterprise/Fortune 500 brands — it is growth-stage, owner-led businesses. Most rigorous agency-client research (ANA/4As, Setup/CMO Institute) is gathered from larger-brand marketers and bigger agencies. This is **adjacent evidence**, not direct evidence of CMS's exact buyer. It is still the best available signal on agency-client relationship dynamics in the Western market, and the underlying human/relational patterns (trust, communication, feeling understood) are unlikely to be exclusive to enterprise buyers — but this scope limitation should be treated as a standing caveat throughout.

## Q1 — Why Do Clients Hire Agencies?

🟢 **Capacity and time, not just skill, is a primary driver.** Independent SMB research (not agency-specific, but describing the buying population CMS targets) shows small business owners are structurally time-poor: roughly half of small businesses handle all of their own marketing internally, the majority spend 10 hours a week or less on it, and a large share cite lack of time and lack of budget as their top marketing constraints.<br>*(Constant Contact/Ascend2 2024–2025; Fiverr Small Business Month Survey 2025, ~6,000 respondents across 25 countries; Taradel 2025)*

🟡 **Owners increasingly outsource specific marketing functions rather than the whole function.** Fiverr's 2025 survey found nearly half of small business owners (46%) use freelance/outside talent for their primary business, most often for visual brand work (45%) and strategic marketing services (28%) — suggesting the "hire" decision is frequently partial/functional, not a full agency handoff.

🟠 **Confidence gaps drive the search for outside help.** SMB research shows a widening gap between marketing activity and marketing confidence: by 2025 only 18% of small business owners described themselves as "very confident" their marketing was working, down from 27% the year before (Constant Contact/Ascend2). This uncertainty — not simply workload — appears to be a trigger for seeking outside expertise, which aligns directly with CMS's "outgrown DIY" hypothesis.

🟡 **B2B-agency literature frames the decision around specialization and bandwidth, not labor replacement.** Agency-side sources (OneIMS, CMD Agency, Americaneagle) consistently frame the value proposition as access to specialized expertise, an outside point of view, and capacity the internal team lacks — this is vendor-authored content, so treat as the industry's own self-description rather than client-reported evidence.

**Synthesis for CMS:** The evidence base for *why clients hire* is stronger on the SMB/owner side (time, confidence, specific-skill gaps) than on formal agency-commissioned research (which mostly studies retention/exit, not acquisition). This matches CMS's own Phase 2 hypothesis that the trigger is less "I need content" and more "I've run out of time/certainty to do this myself."

## Q2 — Why Do Clients Leave Agencies?

This question has the strongest, most direct evidence base of the seven — largely due to one longitudinal source.

### 🟢 Setup Marketing Relationship Survey (in partnership with The CMO Institute) — 8 consecutive years, 400+ brand/agency professionals in the most recent wave

This is the most rigorous, repeated, named study found that directly asks "why do clients end agency relationships" on both sides of the table.

| Year | #1 reason clients report | What agencies believed instead |
|---|---|---|
| 2019–2020 | Budget cuts (24% of clients agreed) | 75% of agencies believed budget was the top reason |
| 2022 | Dissatisfaction with **strategic approach** (43%) | Agencies still pointed to budget cuts (client-ranked budget cuts 8th) |
| 2023 | Dissatisfaction with **value** (up from 39%→53%) | — |
| 2024 | Dissatisfaction with **delivery** (48%, +14 pts YoY) | — |
| 2025 | **Delivery (61%) and Value (61%), tied** | 75% of agencies still cited budget cuts; only 18% cited delivery; only 3% cited "didn't understand our business" |

**Full 2025 client-reported ranking:**
1. Dissatisfaction with delivery — 61%
2. Dissatisfaction with value — 61%
3. Agency did not understand our business — 44%
4. Dissatisfaction with the relationship — 41%
5. Dissatisfaction with strategic approach — 41%
6. Budget cuts — 39%
7. Change in client leadership — 35%
8. Change in agency personnel — 28%

**The central finding, repeated across all 8 years:** clients consistently attribute departures to the agency's own performance and understanding of the business — not external circumstance — while agencies consistently misdiagnose the cause as budget or client-side change. This is described in the source as a "structural blindness," not a one-off gap.

*(Source: Setup®/CMO Institute Marketing Relationship Survey, 2018–2026 waves; setup.us)*

### 🟡 Digiday Research (client-side marketer survey, n=73)
77% of client-side marketers said underperforming/low-quality campaigns were a key reason for ending an agency relationship; 45% cited moving to a cheaper alternative; 25% cited cultural misalignment; agency personnel turnover was also cited as a driver of inefficiency and cost.

### 🟢 ANA/4As joint research (industry bodies representing 20,000+ brands and 600+ agencies)
- The 2023–2024 "Cost of the Pitch" studies found the *value* question (not just cost) is now central to whether relationships continue — value, trust, cost, transparency, and respect were identified as the five defining themes of the relationship.
- The 2025 ANA/4As Client-Agency AOR Tenure study found average relationship tenure has more than doubled since 2016 (from 3.2 years to ~7 years), which the authors attribute to a broader shift toward valuing long-term, trust-based partnerships over transactional/reviewed relationships. The same body of research found that agencies subject to mandatory periodic review are retained for a shorter average period (3.8 years) than those without mandatory review cycles (8.1 years) — implying that the review process itself can accelerate churn, independent of performance.

### 🟠 Anecdotal / practitioner accounts (lower confidence, directionally consistent)
Reddit/LinkedIn/forum-sourced practitioner accounts (Alignable, LinkedIn, State of Digital) consistently and independently raise the same two complaints found in the formal surveys above: (1) the agency didn't take time to understand the business beyond a brief, and (2) slow or reactive communication signaled the client wasn't a priority.

**⚠️ Note on an unverifiable claim:** Several SEO/content-marketing blog sources (Agiled, Focus Digital, OnboardMap) cite a "HubSpot 2025 Agency Retention Report" claiming 73% of departing clients cite poor communication. This report could not be independently located or verified against HubSpot's own published research and is **excluded** from this document's evidence base (🔴 Unverified). It is flagged here only so it is not mistakenly reintroduced later as if it were confirmed.

## Q3 — What Makes a Client Feel That an Agency Is Valuable?

🟡 **Strategic thinking and honest communication outrank creative output.** Campaign (trade press) and Worldwide Partners International surveyed 600+ brand marketers and agency leaders globally: strategic thinking was rated the top driver of relationship value (82%), narrowly ahead of clear/honest communication (81%).

🟡 **Relationship quality is rated above communication and performance as a retention driver by agencies themselves.** One agency-leader survey found 81% cited strong client relationships as the #1 factor in retaining accounts, ahead of clear communication (67%) and campaign performance (49%) (gainapp.com, agency-side self-report — treat as agency perception, not client-reported).

🟢 **Trust is the one variable both sides agree on.** Industry research presented at Cannes Lions (RPA, 2014) found that "importance of trust in the relationship" was the only survey question where agencies and clients gave matching scores — every other dimension of the relationship showed a perception gap between the two sides. This is consistent with the ANA/4As and Setup findings above: agencies and clients broadly agree trust matters, but disagree sharply on whether it currently exists and why it erodes.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Value is judged less on the size of the output and more on (a) whether the agency can be trusted to act in the client's interest, (b) whether the thinking behind the work is visible, and (c) whether communication is honest rather than merely frequent. This directly supports CMS's Sage + Caregiver positioning — but it is *not yet proof* that a strategy-first pitch outperforms an execution-first one commercially; it only shows what practitioners and marketers self-report as mattering to them.

## Q4 — What Makes Clients Feel Misunderstood?

This question has the thinnest formal survey coverage of the seven; the strongest evidence here is qualitative/anecdotal, but it recurs with unusual consistency across independent sources.

🟢 **"Agency did not understand our business" is a top-4 reason for ending the relationship (44%, Setup 2025)** — quantified confirmation that "feeling misunderstood" is not just a soft complaint but a measurable churn driver, ranking above budget cuts and personnel change.

🟠 **Recurring qualitative pattern across independent sources** (State of Digital's two-part client/agency frustration series; Alignable forum thread among agency owners; LinkedIn practitioner post responding to a viral Reddit thread on agency complaints): clients describe feeling misunderstood specifically when:
- the agency treats the account like a templated deliverable rather than learning the business's internal goals, KPIs, and constraints;
- there is no real onboarding/discovery process, so the agency is executing from a brief rather than context;
- account staff turnover forces the client to re-explain the business repeatedly;
- recommendations feel generic/interchangeable with what any competitor's client would receive.

**Synthesis for CMS:** The client-side language used to describe "feeling misunderstood" (generic, templated, brief-driven, not knowing the business beyond the deliverable) maps almost exactly onto the language CMS already uses to describe what it wants to avoid ("diagnosis before prescription," "not a templated package"). This is a genuine point of alignment between the evidence and CMS's existing positioning, not just a coincidence worth flagging with more confidence than the underlying data (mostly 🟠) technically supports.

## Q5 — What Creates Trust?

🟢 **ANA Trust Report (400+ member marketers, 2019)** — the most direct, named study on this exact question:
- Only 29% of client-side marketers rated trust between marketers and agencies as "high"; 55% rated it "moderate"; 17% "low."
- Trust had declined for 28% of respondents over the prior 2–3 years vs. improved for only 13%.
- **Transparency was the single common denominator** across high, moderate, and low-trust relationships — enhanced transparency correlated with high trust; transparency concerns correlated with moderate/low trust.
- This led ANA to form a dedicated "Trust Consortium" to address the issue industry-wide.

🟢 **ANA/4As "Cost of the Pitch II: The Rise of Value" (2024, n=173, all respondents required 3+ years in a direct client-agency relationship)** identified five specific components of trust in the relationship: **trust, value, cost, transparency, and respect** — recommending structured 360° review programs as the mechanism for maintaining it over time, rather than relying on trust as an assumed baseline.

🟢 **Consistency over time builds trust more than any single proof point.** The 2025 ANA/4As tenure study frames the doubling of average relationship length (3.2 → ~7 years since 2016) as evidence that sustained collaboration — not a single pitch, campaign, or credential — is what most reliably signals and builds trust at scale.

🟠 **Proof artifacts (case studies, testimonials, reviews) are consistently cited by agency-side sources as trust-building tools**, but this is drawn from agency marketing/sales advice content (Seven Figure Agency, Waymarks, AgencyAnalytics) rather than from client-reported survey data — i.e., we have strong evidence that agencies *believe* proof artifacts build trust, and much weaker direct evidence of how much weight clients place on them relative to relationship consistency and transparency.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Trust is built primarily through *transparency + consistency over time*, not through any single credibility asset. This is a useful check on any temptation to over-invest in polished proof/testimonial content as the primary trust mechanism — the evidence says sustained, honest delivery matters more than the pitch.

## Q6 — What Creates Frustration?

🟢 **Delivery dissatisfaction is now the single largest driver of client frustration (Setup, 2024–2025)** — rising from a mid-table concern to the #1 or co-#1 reason clients end relationships, a 14-point year-over-year jump in 2024 alone.

🟡 **Overpromising against underdelivering is a named, recurring complaint.** SMB-owner-focused qualitative sources (Surefire Local's compiled small-business-owner interviews) describe a consistent pattern: agencies set lead/result expectations during the sales process that are not met in delivery, and slow response times compound the frustration because it signals the account is not prioritized.

🟠 **Personnel turnover is a secondary but recurring frustration driver** — cited in the Setup survey (28% in 2025), the Digiday survey, and independent practitioner commentary — because it forces clients to repeatedly re-onboard new account staff to their business context, which compounds the "misunderstood" problem in Q4.

🟠 **Generic, cookie-cutter strategy recommendations** not adapted to the specific business are cited across multiple qualitative sources (Surefire Local, State of Digital, Alignable) as a frustration distinct from delivery quality — the work may be executed competently but still feel like it wasn't built *for this business specifically*.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Frustration clusters around three related but distinct failure points: (1) the work itself underperforms relative to what was promised, (2) the relationship feels impersonal/interchangeable, and (3) turnover erodes institutional knowledge of the account. All three map onto the same underlying complaint: **the agency stopped (or never started) operating as if it understood this specific business.**

## Q7 — What Does a Client Actually Mean By "Good Service"?

No single named study asks this exact question in those words; the answer has to be triangulated from what clients report driving satisfaction, retention, and trust across Q2–Q6. Triangulated, "good service" appears to mean, in order of evidenced weight:

1. **Delivery that matches what was promised** — the single largest 2024–2025 churn driver when absent (Setup).
2. **Demonstrable value relative to cost** — tied with delivery as the top reason for leaving in 2025 (Setup); explicitly named as a defining theme of the relationship by ANA/4As.
3. **Evidence the agency understands the specific business**, not just the brief — 44% cite this directly as a reason for leaving (Setup); it is the most consistent theme across all qualitative/anecdotal sources.
4. **Transparent, honest, proactive communication** — the one dimension both agencies and clients agree matters (RPA/Cannes; ANA Trust Report; Campaign/WPI).
5. **Relationship continuity** — consistent account staffing and a long-term orientation rather than a transactional, single-project mindset (ANA/4As tenure study; Setup 2025 personnel-turnover finding).

**Synthesis for CMS:** "Good service" is not primarily a description of output quality — it is a description of *fit between promise and delivery, transparency about that fit, and continuity of understanding over time*. Notably, none of the leading evidence defines "good service" primarily in terms of creative quality, content volume, or platform coverage — which is consistent with CMS's own stated differentiation ("we don't compete on platform coverage or output volume").

## Cross-Question Pattern Summary

| Pattern | Evidence base |
|---|---|
| Agencies and clients diagnose the *same* failures differently — agencies blame external factors, clients blame the agency's own performance and understanding | 🟢 Setup (8-year pattern, every wave measured) |
| "Understanding the business" is both a churn driver (Q2/Q4) and a value driver (Q3) — it is not a soft nice-to-have, it is measurably central to retention | 🟢 Setup 2025 (44%); 🟡 Campaign/WPI |
| Trust is built by transparency and time, not by a single proof asset | 🟢 ANA Trust Report; 🟢 ANA/4As tenure study |
| Delivery/value dissatisfaction has been rising as the #1 driver of churn in the most recent (2024–2025) data — this is a *recent trend*, not a static fact | 🟢 Setup year-over-year data |

## What This Evidence Does *Not* Prove

Consistent with the existing Phase 2 research discipline, this document does not claim that:
- these findings generalize precisely from enterprise/larger-brand marketers to CMS's actual growth-stage owner-led ICP (adjacent evidence, not direct evidence);
- CMS's diagnosis-first model will outperform competitors commercially — only that "being understood" and "delivery matching promise" are what the available research says clients react to;
- any specific proof format (case study, testimonial, etc.) is proven more persuasive than another for this specific audience.
