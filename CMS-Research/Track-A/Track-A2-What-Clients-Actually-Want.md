## Q1 — What Are Clients Buying Beyond Deliverables?

🟢 **Delivery and value, not activity, are what clients say they're actually evaluating.** Setup's 6th Annual Marketing Relationship Survey (400+ brand/agency professionals, 2025) found clients rank dissatisfaction with delivery (61%) and dissatisfaction with value (61%) as the top two reasons relationships end — both ahead of budget cuts (39%). Clients aren't buying tasks; they're buying a promise that gets fulfilled.

🟡 **The market is visibly shifting from hourly/task billing to outcome-based packaging.** Trade-press analysis (Seven Figure Agency, 2025) and Promethean Research's 2026 State of Digital Services report both describe billing models moving away from itemized hours toward packaged outcomes — RSW/US's 2025 Agency Survey found 62% of marketing service firms now productize offerings, with 86% planning to increase this in the next 12 months.

🟡 **"Not knowing what's driving results" is a named SMB pain point, distinct from dissatisfaction with output itself.** Constant Contact research (cited via a marketing-consultancy source) found 23% of small business owners name this as their top marketing pain point — suggesting SMB owners aren't just buying content/posts, they're buying legibility into what's working.

🟠 **Agency-side commentary (2026) frames the shift explicitly as "deliverables to strategy."** Agency Core's 2026 survey of 579 agency leaders and 400 clients (reported via a marketing podcast, not independently verified in full) found 9 in 10 clients say their agency helps them succeed, yet 4 in 10 plan to shrink the relationship within a year — read by the researchers as evidence that routine-deliverable work is increasingly commoditized while strategic contribution is not.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Clients are buying *confidence that the promise will be kept* and *visibility into why it's working* — not the deliverable itself. This directly supports CMS's "Problem → Value → Investment" pricing psychology for Tier 3–4, and validates that Tier 1–2 clients (closer to "Price → Deliverables" psychology per CMS's own framework) are still at elevated risk of feeling like they're buying tasks rather than outcomes unless CMS actively reframes what's being delivered.

## Q2 — Do They Want Execution, Management, Strategy, Expertise, or Partnership?

🟢 **Relationship tenure data suggests the market is moving toward partnership, not transactional engagement.** The ANA/4As Client-Agency AOR Relationship Tenure study (2025) found average client-agency tenure has more than doubled since 2016 — from 3.2 years to ~7 years — which the authors attribute to a broader shift toward valuing long-term, trust-based partnerships over reviewed/transactional relationships.

🟡 **Small business owners are already delegating strategy, not just execution, at meaningful rates.** Intuit QuickBooks (March 2025) found 37% of small businesses have the owner personally driving marketing/advertising strategy, while 54% have a dedicated team or team member driving it — and among the youngest, highest-revenue businesses surveyed, only 8% still have the owner directly overseeing strategy. This tracks a trend line: as a business grows, the "who owns strategy" role moves off the owner's desk — a specific, evidenced version of CMS's own "Client Maturity Progression" (Launch → Growth → Scaling → Maturity) hypothesis.

🟡 **Fiverr's 2025 survey (~6,000 respondents, 25 countries) shows the demand split is genuinely mixed, not monolithic.** Among small business owners already using freelance/outside talent, 45% use it for visual brand execution and 28% for strategic marketing services — meaning execution-only demand and strategy demand coexist rather than one replacing the other. This is consistent with CMS's own Service Depth Spectrum design (Tier 1 execution-only through Tier 4 partnership) rather than contradicting it.

**Synthesis for CMS:** This is not a single answer — it's evidence *for* a spectrum. Clients across the maturity curve want different things at different times, and the market is trending toward valuing sustained partnership at the top of that curve, not one-off execution. This is direct support for CMS's existing Service Depth Spectrum rather than a new finding requiring a strategy change.

## Q3 — How Important Is Specialization?

🟢 **Client preference for specialists over generalists is now measurable and moving fast.** Setup's 2025 survey found only 26% of clients agree that generalists are better than specialists — a 29% decrease from 2024 — and 70% of clients disagree that it makes more sense to work with one generalist agency than several specialists.

🟡 **Industry-wide, specialization is now the default positioning, not the exception.** Promethean Research's 2026 Digital Agency Industry Report found 84% of digital agencies now identify as specialists rather than generalists (per AgencyPro's synthesis of that report). Multiple 2026 agency-economics sources (Haus Advisors, RevenueMemo) independently report niche/specialist agencies earning meaningfully higher margins (40–75% gross margin vs. generalist benchmarks) and command higher fees — a demand-side signal, not just a supply-side trend.

🟠 **Agency-owner commentary (Forbes Agency Council, Sept 2026) frames the mechanism:** generalist agencies "describe what they do," specialists "describe what the client is experiencing" — meaning specialization functions as a proxy for perceived business understanding, not just technical skill.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Specialization is a strong and *increasing* trust/selection signal in the current market — this validates CMS's "Specialists Over Generalists" narrative pillar and "Specialist Network" positioning as commercially current, not just philosophically appealing.

## Q4 — How Important Is Communication?

🟢 **Communication/transparency is a top-cited retention driver, but there's a measurable say-do gap.** AgencyAnalytics' benchmark survey found 36% of agencies cite communication/transparency as the #1 retention factor; a separate 2022 survey found 80% of clients view transparency as crucial, but only 56% report actually experiencing it — a 24-point gap between expectation and delivery.

🟢 **"Understanding my business" and "communication" are increasingly the same complaint from the client's side.** Setup's 2025 data shows "agency did not understand our business" is a top-4 reason for leaving (44%) — this is a communication/discovery failure as much as a competence failure.

🟡 **A general 2026 aggregator (WifiTalents, methodology-disclosed) reports transparent reporting builds trust with 92% of clients** — directionally consistent with the above but from a broader-scope source with less granular sourcing than Setup or ANA/4As.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Communication is not a "soft skill" add-on — it is one of the most frequently cited, most consistently measured retention variables across every source in this research. This validates CMS's "Diagnosis Before Prescription" discovery-first model as directly addressing the #1 and #4 named reasons clients leave providers.

## Q5 — How Important Is Chemistry/Trust?

🟢 **Trust is the one dimension where agencies and clients agree — everywhere else, perception diverges.** RPA's 2014 Cannes Lions research (still cited as foundational by TrinityP3 in 2026) found "importance of trust in the relationship" was the only survey question where agency and client scores matched; every other relationship dimension showed a perception gap.

🟢 **Tenure data indirectly confirms trust's weight.** The ANA/4As tenure study frames sustained collaboration (not a single pitch or credential) as the strongest driver of the doubling in relationship length since 2016 — i.e., trust compounds over time rather than being established once at the point of sale.

🟡 **At the final decision point, chemistry/cultural fit is weighted lower than expected relative to competence signals.** A 2025 MarketingCharts B2B buyer study found industry expertise gets "significant weight" from 52% of buyers at final selection, versus only 20% who give significant weight to cultural/strategic fit and 21% to social proof — even though 85% of buyers separately say understanding their business's challenges is "important" earlier in the process. This is enterprise B2B "hidden buyer" data (adjacent evidence, not SMB-direct), but it's a useful nuance: **trust/chemistry appears to function as a gate to get into consideration, not the deciding factor at final selection** — competence and fit for the specific problem win the final call.

🟠 **Agency-consultant commentary treats chemistry as foundational infrastructure, not a vibe check.** Forrester's ongoing "Got Chemistry?" series and 2026 Creativepool commentary both argue chemistry has to be built deliberately (team fit, decision clarity, governance) rather than assumed to emerge — framing it as a predictor of post-sale performance, not just a pitch-stage feeling.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Trust/chemistry is necessary but not sufficient — it's the entry ticket to being considered credible, but the deciding factor at the point of commitment is demonstrated competence and problem-fit. This has a direct implication for CMS's Sage + Caregiver positioning: the "look out for the client's best interest" framing builds the trust that gets CMS into the conversation, but the close still needs to be won on specialist competence and right-sized fit.

## Q6 — How Important Is Business Understanding?

🟢 **Business understanding is quantified as both a churn driver and a final-selection driver, at meaningful weight.** Setup 2025: 44% cite "agency did not understand our business" as a reason for leaving. MarketingCharts 2025 (B2B "hidden buyers," enterprise-adjacent): 85% consider "understanding of my business's challenges and needs" important, and 75% rate "understanding of trends affecting my industry" as important — both ranking above cultural fit and social proof.

🟡 **An older but still-cited stat puts this even higher for SMB-adjacent contexts:** Setup's original 2019 survey (cited via a secondary source) found 80% of businesses prefer an agency that fully understands their operations — consistent direction with the more recent, higher-confidence data above, though the original source is now several years old and the exact methodology wasn't independently re-verified here.

### Philippine Market Note (🟠 Anecdotal — Secondary ICP)

No named PH survey directly measures "business understanding" as a selection criterion the way Setup or MarketingCharts do for Western markets. However, several 2026 Philippine agency-advisory sources converge on the same theme in less rigorous form:

- 🟠 A 2026 Philippine agency-selection guide (ACX Hub) lists "no written strategy or roadmap after the first one or two months," "heavy use of jargon instead of straightforward explanations," and lack of understanding of "Filipino buying behaviour, regional differences, and mixed online/offline journeys" as explicit red flags business owners are advised to watch for — implying local buyers already treat business/context understanding as a trust signal, consistent with the Western data. This is agency-authored advisory content, not a client-reported survey.
- 🟠 A separate 2026 PH-market guide (Lime Digital Asia) frames the freelancer-vs-agency choice explicitly around depth of understanding ("a freelancer usually brings deep skill in one area") vs. breadth — echoing the Western specialization pattern (Q3), again from vendor-authored commentary rather than a client survey.
- 🟠 A 2026 Philippine tool-stack comparison (365Outsource, citing SEO.com.ph and Jotform pricing/partner data) notes Philippine freelancers charge as low as ₱5,000/month while agency retainers for PH SMBs typically run $2,500–$12,000/month — framed by the source as buyers "coming out ahead on total cost of ownership" specifically because of platform-partner access and dedicated support, i.e., buyers are implicitly paying for infrastructure/credibility, not just labor.

**Synthesis for CMS (including PH note):** "Understanding the business" is the single most consistently supported finding across Track A1 and A2 combined — it shows up as a churn driver (A1), a value driver (A1 Q3), and a separately-quantified selection criterion (A2 Q6). It is CMS's single most evidence-backed differentiation claim for the Western market. The Philippine-market echo of this pattern is directionally consistent but meaningfully weaker in evidence quality (vendor-authored guides rather than client-reported survey data). This should be logged as a **research gap**: CMS does not yet have a PH-equivalent of the Setup/ANA-4As/MarketingCharts survey base for A2, and any PH-specific claims in future brand or sales material should be labeled as directional inference, not measured finding, until better-sourced local research exists.

## Cross-Question Pattern Summary

| Pattern | Evidence base |
|---|---|
| Specialization preference is *rising*, not static — a 29-point YoY swing away from "generalists are better" | 🟢 Setup 2025 |
| Communication and "business understanding" are functionally the same complaint from the client's side | 🟢 Setup 2025; 🟢 AgencyAnalytics |
| Trust/chemistry gates consideration; competence/fit wins the final decision | 🟢 RPA/TrinityP3; 🟡 MarketingCharts (enterprise-adjacent) |
| Demand is genuinely split across the execution→partnership spectrum, not converging on one mode | 🟡 Fiverr 2025; 🟡 QuickBooks 2025 |
| The market is structurally repricing around outcomes/packages rather than hours or tasks | 🟡 RSW/US 2025; Promethean 2026 |

## What This Evidence Does *Not* Prove

- That CMS's specific Service Depth Spectrum (4-tier) outperforms other tiering models commercially
- That Filipino or Western SMB owners specifically (vs. the enterprise/mid-market respondents in most of this data) weight these factors identically
- That specialization alone overcomes a price disadvantage
- That "chemistry" can be manufactured rather than genuinely built
- That the Philippine-market pattern noted above generalizes beyond the small set of anecdotal sources cited

## Pricing/Positioning Cross-Reference (Supplementary — Not a Direct A2 Answer)

This section does not answer an A2 audience-psychology question directly. It is included as supplementary context because it is load-bearing for `Pricing-Rate-Research-CMS.md` and for how CMS talks about its Service Depth Spectrum.

🟡 **The market is actively repricing around specialization and outcomes, and specialists are capturing the margin.** Promethean Research's 2026 Digital Agency Industry Report (via AgencyPro's synthesis) puts specialist positioning at 84% of digital agencies industry-wide. Haus Advisors' 2026 benchmark compilation cites specialist agencies targeting $250,000+ revenue per employee vs. a $180,000+ target for generalists (sourced to agency consultant Sakas & Company). RevenueMemo's 2026 analysis reports niche agencies achieving 40–75% gross margins vs. compressed margins for generalist positioning.

🟡 **Billing models are shifting away from hours/line-items toward packaged outcomes.** RSW/US's 2025 Agency Survey found 62% of marketing service firms now productize their offerings, with 86% planning to increase this in the next 12 months.

**Why this matters for CMS specifically:** This is independent, current (2025–2026) market confirmation of two things CMS's existing Pricing Rate Research document already recommends: (1) keeping granular/hourly pricing internal and presenting simplified, outcome-framed tiers to clients, and (2) treating Tier 3–4 as "Problem → Value → Investment" rather than itemized pricing. The specialist-margin data also gives CMS a defensible, cited reason (not just a values-based one) to hold price floors on specialist-network positioning rather than competing on generalist/commodity rates.
