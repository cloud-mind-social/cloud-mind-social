# Cloud Mind Social; competitor research and SWOT

Research date: October 14, 2026; Asia/Manila  
Track: Brand & Strategy  
Status: Research draft for Seul's review; recommendations are not approved offer changes.  
Purpose: Identify young and small providers competing for similar work; assess services, advertised rates, target buyers and observable lead routes; convert findings into CMS decisions and tests.

## What the research establishes

CMS faces competitors that already promise strategic direction, tailored support, founder attention and business outcomes. Those phrases alone offer little protection. A stronger competitive position would make the diagnosis visible; show the decision it helps an owner make; and demonstrate dependable delivery with permissioned evidence.

This is a purposive shortlist, not a market census or price survey. A recently founded provider is not automatically equal to CMS in revenue, capacity or traction. No private financials, customer counts or sales conversion data were accessible. Founding dates below are published statements, not independently checked incorporation records.

## Evidence rules

* 🟢 High: directly observed public page text, price label, offer or link. This proves what is advertised; it does not verify service quality or claimed outcomes.
* 🟡 Moderate: a directory-listed company attribute, a stated date with conflicting wording, or a reasoned interpretation of positioning.
* 🟠 Anecdotal: isolated testimonial evidence; not used to establish comparative business performance.
* 🔴 Unverified: private funnel steps, financial health, conversion rates, claimed results without underlying records; excluded from firm findings.

SWOT interpretations are hypotheses. A public-page weakness is a buyer-facing disclosure gap, not proof of an internal operational failure. An inaccessible page is recorded as a research limitation, not automatically a broken customer experience. Funnel sequences below describe available routes; they do not prove that every visitor follows that order.

### 1. PALEONO; Philippines

[Website and pricing](https://paleono.com/)

**Fit:** Provisional emerging specialist; exact age and team unknown. The site says real client work is forthcoming and labels its campaigns as mock work. **Buyer:** Aesthetic clinic owners, including aesthetic dental clinics. **Offer:** Facebook/Instagram management, 8–12 feed/story posts monthly, captions, calendar, paid campaign management, creatives, DM/community handling and monthly review/report. **Rate:** ₱25,000/month; month to month. Form submission was not tested. **Disclosure gap:** Instagram navigates to an internal /Instagram.com path; Facebook returns to the site, so neither identifies a verified social account. [Source](https://paleono.com/).

**SWOT interpretation:** S; specific buyer and a simple offer. W; limited demonstrated client proof. O; a narrow specialty could make relevant evidence easier to build. T; buyers may prefer established clinic specialists. **CMS gain:** Publish source-backed portfolio work with accurate production and publication states; give each diagnostic a concrete buyer and decision. Treat the mock campaign's result language as hypothetical, never as achieved performance.

## CMS SWOT; research interpretation

| Quadrant | Finding | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | Approved audience, diagnosis-led positioning and bounded lead ladder provide a coherent strategic foundation | Translate each concept into a useful customer decision; planning quality alone is not buyer proof |
| Strength | Grounded human perspective and truth controls support responsible content and reporting | Show sources, claim limits and exact output states in selected portfolio examples |
| Strength | Finite execution and operations services are allowed within the reviewed architecture | Address a defined need without accepting an oversized recurring commitment |
| Weakness | The reviewed pricing source remains a draft; scope, margin and qualification rules are open in that source | Finalize one testable scope with delivery estimates and paid evidence; do not promote internal reference bands as final prices |
| Weakness | This review does not establish current capacity, response coverage, prospect demand or conversion baselines | Verify these before selling ongoing responsibility; treat them as unresolved evidence, not proven failures |
| Weakness | Strategic Social Intelligence requires explanation in the buyer's language | Pair the descriptor with a tangible deliverable and the decision it supports |
| Opportunity | Some reviewed offers leave platform counts, ad spend, currency or exact boundaries unclear | Make these easy to inspect in CMS's offer card and proposal |
| Opportunity | Specialist positioning makes the business context immediately recognizable | Test one subsegment within the approved Western expertise/service beachhead without rewriting the ICP |
| Opportunity | Free consultations are common; a useful bounded diagnostic could establish judgment earlier | Show a permissioned or clearly hypothetical sample decision brief; avoid giving away a complete strategy |
| Threat | Young and small providers already use strategy, tailored support and outcome language | Build a visible method and specific proof rather than adding more positioning adjectives |
| Threat | Local menus and Western execution offers can anchor prospects to low prices | Explain differences in responsibility and scope; avoid universal currency-converted comparisons |
| Threat | Local providers can capture content on-site; integrated providers offer websites and follow-up systems | Qualify asset availability and operating gaps; accept work within verified capability |
| Threat | Testimonials and polished examples can outweigh abstract planning for a prospect | Present accurate, permitted work and process evidence; never convert mock results into claimed outcomes |

## Actions CMS can use

These are proposed tests, not approved commercial changes. They retain the existing primary market and approved diagnostic ladder.

| Priority | Proposed action | Why this follows | Evidence of useful progress |

| 1 | Create a concise sample Social Clarity Intensive decision brief, using a permissioned real source or a clearly labeled hypothetical | Makes diagnosis inspectable | Qualified owners can explain the decision the brief helps them make |
| 1 | Define one diagnostic scope: inputs, deliverable, delivery time, revisions, fee, exclusions and stop point | Prevents an open-ended audit and creates a reviewable purchase | Actual hours, revision effort, price objections and paid acceptance |
| 1 | Assemble a small proof set; identify CMS as Merille's internal business engagement and respect client display permissions | Competes through visible work while preserving truth controls | Sources, contribution, approval, delivery and publication states traceable for each piece |

| 2 | Use a short qualification intake covering offer, audience, existing social setup, previous attempts, business goal, budget and urgency | Separates vague interest from a diagnosable opportunity | Qualified submissions divided by all submissions in a defined cohort; disclose reviewed/unreviewed counts |

### Proposed CMS lead route; existing planning direction, operational status unverified

Platform-specific observation or sourced perspective; Social Signal Check for cold/problem-aware owners; bounded Signal-to-Strategy Review for qualified interest; paid Social Clarity Intensive for a decision-ready buyer; right-sized execution or strategy engagement. Hot leads may enter directly at the paid diagnostic. The website is the planned owned conversion environment; retain a usable inquiry route and avoid unnecessary early friction. Verify actual destinations and measurement before activation.

Compare qualified inquiry, show rate, paid diagnostic acceptance, follow-on scope fit and delivery effort. Likes and follower counts alone do not establish that a competitor's funnel works or that CMS's offer is validated.

### Pricing interpretation

No market average is calculated. This sample mixes asset production, recurring management, local shooting, broad digital services, hourly directory rates and custom quotes; currencies, media spend and taxes are not consistently disclosed. No exchange-rate conversions are used. A published price is an advertised offer, not evidence of transactions at that price.

The Phase 2.5.6 source contains internal CMS working bands, including Western Presence at $999–$1,500/month and a Social Clarity Intensive pilot at $300–$500. Those remain unapproved public prices in that source. This research does not validate them. Test price against the actual scope, delivery effort and buyer acceptance; retain separate Western and Philippine reference cards.

## Recheck checklist for a future competitor update

Record verified official links; founding/team statements and conflicts; exact package date and currency; taxes and media budget; platform and asset counts; asset supply and shoot conditions; response coverage; reporting; revision/contract terms; lead capture; declared follow-up; and permissioned proof. Mark unknowns explicitly. Never infer ads, revenue, buyer validation or funnel performance from a polished page.
