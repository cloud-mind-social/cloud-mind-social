# Pricing Rate Research Project — Cloud Mind Social
### A Consolidated Market Rate Study for Service Pricing, Tiering, and Client Segmentation

This document consolidates a multi-round web research project benchmarking Cloud Mind Social's (CMS) full service menu against real market rates — international/global freelancer and agency pricing, and Philippine-specific freelancer and agency pricing — segmented across CMS's Local (Filipino), Asian (regional), and Western (US/UK/AU) client targets as defined in `Target-Market-Research-CMS.md`.

**Purpose:** Give CMS a defensible, research-backed rate card for every service on the agency menu, organized by the existing Service Depth Spectrum (Fast Food → Casual Dining → Fine Dining → Private Chef), while flagging which figures are directly market-sourced versus reasonably estimated from adjacent data where no direct market SKU exists.

**How to use this document:** Internal pricing/quoting reference first. Client-facing proposals should still follow CMS's core principle — *"Don't quote a Fine Dining or Private Chef client a line-item price sheet — quote them an outcome."* Tier 1–2 clients respond to Price → Deliverables; Tier 3–4 clients respond to Problem → Value → Investment.

## 1. Currency & Scaling Methodology

- **Base exchange rate used throughout:** ₱58 = $1 USD
- **PH-scaling logic:** Where no direct Philippine-market rate exists, figures are scaled down from global/US benchmarks using the ~70–85% cost-reduction ratio consistently observed across every directly-comparable PH vs. Western data point in this research (e.g., PH agencies serving Western clients price at 70–82% below US agency rates; offshore personal branding providers execute at 70–80% below US rates).
- **Confidence flagging:** Each section notes whether pricing is (a) **Directly Sourced** — a real published/quoted market rate exists, or (b) **Estimated** — calculated from adjacent market data because no direct SKU exists for that exact service. This distinction matters for CMS's Sage archetype positioning ("backed by process, data, not hype") — internal teams should know which numbers are defensible in a client conversation and which are working estimates pending real engagement data.

## 11. Strategic Recommendations Summary

1. **Never quote at the marketplace floor.** Every PH-specific source in this research explicitly frames sub-₱5,000/month or $5–20/post pricing as exploitative "race to the bottom" work. CMS has a citable, values-aligned reason to hold price floors even when local clients push back.

2. **Bundle-only services should stay bundled.** Content batching coordination, branded hashtag setup, social listening reports (Tier 2), and team/specialist management, board reporting (Tier 4) have no standalone market precedent — forcing an artificial à la carte price on these creates client confusion rather than clarity.

3. **The freelancer-to-agency price jump is smallest for local clients, largest for Western clients.** This confirms CMS's own cultural research: Western clients pay a premium for packaging/reliability even at steep discounts to their home market, while local Filipino clients see a much narrower freelancer-to-agency gap.

4. **Reserve granular per-second/per-slide pricing for internal quoting only.** Client-facing menus should use simplified named tiers to avoid line-item nitpicking.

5. **CMS's Tier 3–4 pricing philosophy should remain outcome-based, not itemized**, consistent with the brand's own stated principle. This document exists to give the Founder cost-basis confidence when building bundled proposals — not to become a public price sheet.

6. **Three genuine white-space opportunities identified:** AI content systems setup, infographic design as a named specialty, and community-led growth strategy packaging — none have mature competitor pricing yet, giving CMS room to define the category locally.
