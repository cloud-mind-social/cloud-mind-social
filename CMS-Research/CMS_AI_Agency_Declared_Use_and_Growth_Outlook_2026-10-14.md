## What about those keeping it discreet?

A 2026 study of freelance workers and clients found that workers often disclosed AI use only when asked; clients preferred proactive disclosure. This supports the existence of discreet use, but does **not** establish how many agencies quietly operate with one person covering an advertised team.

**Source:** [Hwang et al.; Practices and Policies of AI Disclosure in Freelance Work](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.07459v1), March 8, 2026.  
**Confidence:** 🟢 High for the study's reported findings; 🟡 Moderate for application to CMS. Its freelance participant population does not establish the prevalence of undisclosed agency staffing.

There is also a practical distinction:

* **Service-led messaging:** Public pages emphasize the service; proposals explain the material production method and accountable people.
* **Inflated staffing:** Pages imply separate human researchers, writers and designers who do not exist.

The recommendation for CMS is the first approach. CMS can protect its prompts and workflows while being accurate about staffing.

## Assessment for CMS

This is a viable operating model to test. The strongest growth opportunity is to solve a specific owner problem through a profitable, repeatable scope. More AI output only helps when clients buy the work, accept its quality and continue the relationship.

For CMS's own projection, use **retained clients × average monthly fee** to calculate recurring monthly revenue; then account for delivery costs, revisions and available hours. Project fees and other revenue require separate entries. Until those inputs are measured, a revenue scenario is useful; a promised growth percentage is not.

This is a strategic interpretation, not evidence of CMS buyer validation, established capacity or a guaranteed premium.
