Each finding is tagged with a confidence level based on the source type (same legend as Track A1/A2):

| Tag | Meaning |

## Q1 — What Does "ROI" Mean to Clients?

🟢 **"ROI" doesn't mean one thing — it's whatever metric a team already trusts.** Sprout Social's 2025 Impact of Social Media Report found marketing leaders define social ROI inconsistently: 68% point to engagement, 65% to conversion, 57% to revenue, 55% to efficiency, and 51% to discoverability to define social ROI. Only teams rated "expert" in measurement consistently use revenue/efficiency metrics — most teams default to engagement and conversion.

🟡 **Belief in social's value and the ability to define/prove it are two separate things.** DesignRush's 2026 Benchmark Survey of marketing leaders found roughly 90% are confident social contributes to revenue (65.5% "very confident," 24.1% "somewhat confident"), but only 35% can clearly attribute revenue back to social activity.

🟡 **For SMBs specifically, "ROI" collapses into "sales/revenue" as the practical, working definition.** LocaliQ's 2026 Small Business Marketing Trends Report found sales and revenue is the top-rated "very important" metric (78%), ahead of conversion rate and cost-per-lead — both of which are metrics with a direct, intuitive line back to revenue rather than engagement.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Clients aren't applying a technical ROI formula — they're asking "did this make me money?" and reaching for whatever number feels closest to that answer. This means CMS's reporting has to translate engagement/awareness metrics into revenue-adjacent language, not assume clients will do that translation themselves.

## Q2 — What Results Do They Actually Expect From Social?

🟢 **In the most recent, largest, most directly-relevant Western SMB sample, social is now expected to be the top revenue driver — not just a brand-awareness channel.** Constant Contact's "Small Business Now" report (February 2026, 1,500+ small-business owners across Australia, Canada, New Zealand, UK, and US — via EMARKETER) found 68% of small-business owners say social media posting and paid ads will drive the most value for their business in 2026, ranking above every other channel measured.

🟡 **Named-objective data confirms sales, not awareness, is the primary expectation.** Taradel's 2025 SMB survey found 55.6% of respondents name increasing sales as their top marketing objective, versus 19.2% for lead generation and just 12% for brand awareness.

🟡 **A conflicting data point exists and should be flagged rather than smoothed over.** Sprout Social's 2025 data (a different sample, different question framing) found 60% of marketers say their primary social goal is brand awareness. The likely explanation: Taradel asked owners about their overall marketing objective, while Sprout Social asked specifically about social's role within a broader marketing mix — owners may still see social as an awareness/discovery layer even while expecting the total marketing effort to produce sales.

🟡 **Expectations and reported results diverge.** The Small Business Expo Research Desk (2026, n=363) found 52.9% of small businesses call social "very important" to sales and another 14.0% call it "critical" — but the same respondents describe their actual results as unpredictable, inconsistent, or only somewhat effective.

**Synthesis for CMS:** The honest expectation-setting problem is real: owners increasingly expect social to be a sales channel, not just an awareness channel, but most SMBs' actual experience with it is inconsistent. This is a direct opening for CMS's diagnosis-first model — setting the right expectation (what social *can* realistically deliver at each tier) before the engagement starts.

## Q3 — How Do Clients Judge Whether Marketing Is Worth Paying For?

🟢 **They judge it by whether it can be connected to the bottom line — and that connection is hardest in exactly the kind of business CMS itself is.** The CMO Survey (Duke University's Fuqua School of Business, co-sponsored by Deloitte and the American Marketing Association; Spring 2025 edition, 281 marketing leaders, 99% VP-level or higher; the longest-running marketing leadership survey, founded 2008) found demonstrating marketing's impact on financial outcomes is hardest in **professional services (87% report difficulty)** — the highest of any sector measured — compared to just 26.7% in retail/wholesale. B2B companies (65%) struggle more than B2C companies (50%), and companies with zero online sales (70%) struggle more than pure-play internet companies (57%).

🟢 **Pressure to prove worth is rising sharply and coming from multiple directions.** The same CMO Survey line found 52% of marketing leaders report increased CFO pressure to prove marketing's value, up from 45% in 2021.

🟡 **This pressure has intensified further in the most recent data available.** A November 2025 secondary analysis of The CMO Survey (Funnel.io) reports board-level pressure up 21% and CEO pressure up 20% between 2023 and 2025 — directionally consistent with, and building on, the primary CMO Survey trend line above.

**Synthesis for CMS:** This is a direct, evidence-backed reason why CMS's clients specifically struggle to judge value: professional/B2B service businesses — a large share of CMS's likely client base — sit in the hardest-to-measure category identified in this research. This isn't a CMS-specific failure to communicate; it's a structural measurement problem in the category CMS's clients operate in, which reinforces the case for CMS proactively building simple, revenue-adjacent reporting rather than assuming clients will intuit the connection on their own.

## Q4 — What Metrics Do They Understand?

🟢 **Sales/revenue, conversion rate, and cost-per-lead are the metrics SMBs already trust — because they map directly onto numbers the owner already tracks elsewhere in the business.** LocaliQ's 2026 report found sales/revenue rated "very important" by 78% of small businesses, with conversion rate and cost-per-lead named as the next tier of directly-tied, well-understood metrics tied to advertising performance.

🟡 **Lead-volume comparisons are a commonly cited, easily grasped framing, though the sourcing trail is secondhand.** Widely-repeated statistics (traced to HubSpot, cited via a 2026 aggregator, not independently re-verified here) claim businesses using social media generate roughly double the leads of those that don't, with a notably higher lead-to-close rate than outbound methods. Treat the exact multipliers as unverified, but the underlying pattern — leads and lead-to-close rate as an understood, trusted metric category — is consistent with the LocaliQ finding above.

**Synthesis for CMS:** Clients understand metrics that already exist in their non-marketing vocabulary: sales, leads, cost-per-lead, conversion rate. Anything that requires the client to learn a new unit of measurement (engagement rate, reach, impressions) starts at a trust deficit before the number is even reported.

## Q5 — What Metrics Do They Distrust?

🟢 **Followers, likes, views, and impressions are increasingly distrusted as standalone indicators — and there's now a measurable, named reason why: bot and invalid traffic.** Swydo's 2026 agency-reporting guide, citing Imperva's 2025 Bad Bot Report and Pixalate's 2025 Invalid Traffic (IVT) benchmarks, states plainly that "any metric that doesn't filter for invalid traffic is more vanity in 2026 than it was in 2022" — meaning the same follower or impression number that would have signaled real growth a few years ago no longer reliably represents human activity.

🟠 **The "vanity metrics are dead" narrative is now nearly universal across 2026 marketing commentary** (Improvado, Nutshell, Swydo, and multiple agency blogs) — follower count, likes, and raw impressions are consistently named as the metrics clients and marketers alike are being told not to trust, in favor of saves, shares, conversions, and revenue-adjacent numbers.

🟠 **There's a real say-do gap on this exact point, worth flagging rather than smoothing over.** A 2026 influencer-marketing statistics compilation found 67.3% of marketers worldwide still name follower count as their *main* criterion for selecting influencer partners — far ahead of engagement rate (14.1%) — despite years of industry messaging that follower count is a poor signal. Distrust of vanity metrics is now the stated industry norm, but decision-making behavior hasn't fully caught up.

**Synthesis for CMS:** The metrics clients say they distrust (followers, likes, impressions) are often still the ones most visibly displayed on dashboards and most commonly used in practice — which means CMS has an opportunity to differentiate simply by *not* leading with those numbers in client reporting, while being aware that some clients may still instinctively reach for follower count as a comfort metric even after being told not to.

## Q6 — Why Do Clients Struggle to See Social-Media Value?

🟢 **The customer journey genuinely doesn't isolate cleanly to one channel, and this is a named, structural measurement problem, not a client misunderstanding.** Improvado's 2026 guide describes the core mechanic: a buyer might see a social post, click a search ad, receive an email, and then purchase — isolating social's specific contribution requires attribution modeling most SMBs (and many enterprise teams) don't have in place, and each platform reports its own metrics in its own format, in its own dashboard.

🟢 **Most marketers, at every level, cannot actually calculate accurate ROI at all — this is not unique to social or to CMS's clients.** Sender.net's 2025–2026 benchmark compilation found only 36% of marketers say they can accurately measure ROI, and 47% struggle to measure ROI across multiple channels specifically because attribution is genuinely difficult — not because of a knowledge gap that better reporting alone would fix.

🟡 **Confidence and attribution ability are diverging over time, not converging.** DesignRush's 2026 survey (cited under Q1) found the gap between belief (90% confident social contributes to revenue) and proof (35% can attribute it) — meaning the value problem isn't that clients don't believe social works, it's that almost nobody in the industry can currently show the specific mechanism by which it worked.

🟠 **The Philippine market shows the identical concern in owner-facing language, though from a vendor-authored rather than survey source.** A 2026 Philippine social media marketing guide (Sirmarketer) states plainly that "one of the most common concerns Philippine business owners have about social media marketing is whether they can measure the actual return on their investment" — directionally consistent with the Western pattern but not independently survey-verified.

**Synthesis for CMS:** This connects directly back to Track A1's Constant Contact finding (only 18% of small business owners describe themselves as "very confident" their marketing is working, down from 27% the year before). The pattern across A1, A2, and now A3 is consistent: it's not that clients can't understand social media's value in principle — it's that the industry-wide attribution infrastructure to *prove* it doesn't reliably exist yet, at any business size. This is a structural gap CMS can turn into a differentiator by being explicit about what it can and can't attribute, rather than overpromising clean revenue-attribution the way much of the category implicitly does.

## Cross-Question Pattern Summary

| Pattern | Evidence base |
|---|---|
| ROI is defined by whichever metric a team already trusts, not a standard formula | 🟢 Sprout Social 2025; 🟡 LocaliQ 2026 |
| Expectation has shifted from "be visible" to "drive sales" — but results remain inconsistent | 🟢 Constant Contact/EMARKETER 2026; 🟡 Small Business Expo 2026 |
| Professional/B2B services — CMS's own client category — sit in the hardest-to-measure segment industry-wide | 🟢 The CMO Survey, Spring 2025 (Duke/Deloitte/AMA) |
| Belief that social works is high; ability to prove it is low, and the gap is well-documented | 🟢 DesignRush 2026; 🟢 Sender.net |
| Vanity-metric distrust is now the stated industry norm, but behavior hasn't fully caught up | 🟢 Swydo 2026; 🟠 TechnologyChecker.io 2025 |

## What This Evidence Does *Not* Prove

- That CMS's own reporting/attribution approach would outperform the industry-wide 36% "can accurately measure ROI" baseline
- That Filipino SMB owners weight these same metrics identically to Western owners (PH evidence here is thin and anecdotal)
- That any single metric (revenue, leads, etc.) is sufficient on its own to satisfy a client's sense of "worth it"
- That clients' stated distrust of vanity metrics reliably changes their actual purchasing/renewal decisions
