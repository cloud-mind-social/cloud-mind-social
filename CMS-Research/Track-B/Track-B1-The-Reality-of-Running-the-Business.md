## Related Track A Evidence

Track A established that fragmented agency/client relationships, weak business understanding, and the need for clearer strategic guidance are recurring market problems. B1 tests the **owner side** of that environment: whether operational overload helps explain why social media becomes fragmented, deprioritized, or reactively delegated.

---

# Core Question

> What does day-to-day business reality look like for the owner CMS wants to serve?

The strongest B1 finding is:

> **The owner is often still part of the operating machinery while simultaneously being expected to make growth decisions. Marketing competes with operations for owner attention.**

---

# 1. Owner attention is fragmented across core business work

🟢 **High**

U.S. Chamber of Commerce small-business research shows owners devote significant time to administrative and compliance work in addition to revenue-generating activity. In Q4 2024, 73% reported spending a great deal or fair amount of time on taxes and 73% on recordkeeping; payroll, licensing/permits, and cybersecurity/privacy also consumed substantial attention.

Subsequent U.S. Chamber waves continued to show increased compliance burden, including 40% of small businesses reporting increased compliance time/resources in Q2 2025 and 37% in Q2 2026.

**Implication:** Social media is not competing only with other marketing tasks. It is competing with finance, staffing, customers, compliance, delivery, and administration.

---

# 2. Labour and staffing constraints pull owners back into operations

🟢 **High**

Statistics Canada Q2 2025 reported that among businesses facing labour-related obstacles, 46.4% expected management to work increased hours; the figure reached 68.1% in professional, scientific, and technical services. Nearly 40% expected labour obstacles to limit business growth.

**Implication:** Even when the owner intends to focus on growth, staffing gaps can force them back into operating roles.

---

# 3. Cost, demand, staffing, and complexity are simultaneous pressures

🟢 **High / 🟡 Moderate**

Canadian official SME research identifies recurring barriers including rising input costs, recruiting/retaining skilled employees, demand fluctuations, tax burden, and labour shortages.

These pressures matter because marketing decisions are made inside this wider operating environment. A business owner can believe social media is important and still deprioritize it when cash flow, staffing, customer service, or fulfilment becomes more urgent.

---

# 4. Day-to-day work can crowd out growth work

🟡 **Moderate**

Canadian ISED small-business research has repeatedly described the risk of owners spending too much time on day-to-day operations, reducing attention available for planning and growth.

This supports a useful distinction:

- **Working in the business:** delivery, admin, customer handling, fulfilment, staffing
- **Working on the business:** strategy, positioning, marketing systems, growth planning

CMS's audience may be trying to do both simultaneously.

---

# 5. The evidence supports broad internal-capacity gaps, but not a universal social-media skill gap

🟡 **Moderate**

Current Western SMB research supports limited internal capacity and expertise in many functions. It does **not** justify claiming that most growth-stage owner-led businesses specifically lack social-media knowledge.

The safer finding is:

> **The owner may lack time, specialist depth, or internal capacity even when they understand the basic importance of social media.**

---

# 6. Owners often remain operationally involved

🟡 **Moderate**

Across official small-business research, owners and managers frequently remain directly involved in staffing, finance, customer issues, compliance, and delivery. Exact task mixes differ heavily by industry and business size, so CMS should not create a single stereotyped owner routine.

---

# 7. Delegation is not automatically easy

🟡 **Moderate**

Older ISED work and broader small-business literature suggest delegation can be constrained by control concerns, hiring difficulty, training requirements, and lack of reliable internal systems.

What cannot yet be stated confidently is the prevalence of delegation difficulty specifically for social media among CMS's exact ICP.

---

# 8. Stress is part of the operating environment

🟡 **Moderate**

Peer-reviewed entrepreneur and small-business research links owner stress to workload, liquidity, administrative burden, income uncertainty, staffing, and difficult customers.

B1 should not over-medicalize the audience. The relevant conclusion is operational:

> **Marketing decisions are often made under time pressure and competing business demands.**

---

# 9. Growth means business outcomes, not social metrics

🟢 **High / 🟡 Moderate**

Official and survey evidence consistently frames business growth in terms such as revenue, customers, staffing/capacity, investment, and resilience.

For CMS this means:

> **Social media is a mechanism for growth, not the objective itself.**

Followers, reach, and engagement may matter, but they are intermediate measures unless tied to business outcomes.

---

# Core B1 Finding

> **The owner is often still part of the operating machinery while expected to make growth decisions. Marketing competes with operations for owner attention.**

This is the foundation for later Track B questions about social ownership, outsourcing, frustration, and buying triggers.

---

# What This Evidence Does Not Prove

- that all CMS prospects are overwhelmed;
- that all owners personally manage social media;
- that social media is always the first function deprioritized;
- that lack of social skill is the dominant problem;
- one universal owner schedule or operating pattern.
