## 1. Recommendation and commercial purpose

Use the website as the resource-delivery and lead-record location. Send people to the exact resource page they requested. Do not send them to the homepage and make them search.

Recommended standard journey: relevant keyword comment; one private message with a resource-page link; a short email form; immediate resource access; an optional qualification question; further useful email only when the person opts in.

The initial private message can contain the website link directly. That satisfies the user's proposed flow with less friction. Do not add a compulsory “say YES” or “follow us first” step.

A resource request, an email address, Messenger eligibility, a marketing subscription and a qualified sales lead are different states. Keep them separate. More keywords do not mean more qualified demand.

Start with CLARITY, INQUIRY and HANDOFF. Design one reusable website page/form layout and one message framework; do not build 24 independent systems at launch.

### Scope refinements from the previous research

The previous bank included contextual or human-review variants. For an automatic-download MVP, PRIORITY, OFFER, TRUST and WORDS are proposed as owner-completed worksheets, not silently promised personalized advice. PROOF, CLARITY and COMPARE may likewise launch as self-guided utilities. Personalized interpretation stays under a separately bounded review. This is a proposed production refinement; the earlier research source remains unchanged.

REVIEW is a request for human work, not an instant download. SAMPLE is proposed as an open proof-of-method asset with optional lead capture. SIGNAL can later become a scored or guided web check; its first version may be a downloadable self-check. No arbitrary score should be presented as a validated diagnosis.

## 2. Website versus direct download versus access grant

| Route | Suitable use | Capture and friction | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dedicated resource page plus short form | Standard worksheets; CLARITY, INQUIRY, HANDOFF and most reserve resources | Email captured before immediate access; the exchange must be stated before the click. | Default MVP. |
| Direct file download | Open examples; troubleshooting fallback; an expressly ungated offer | A download alone does not capture an email lead. Offer a voluntary email copy or review request. | Use deliberately; do not report anonymous downloads as captured leads. |
| Open resource page | SAMPLE or public method explanation | No forced gate; optional subscription or review invitation afterward. | Builds inspectable trust. |
| Request form followed by human delivery | REVIEW and any explicitly personalized work | Requires relevant business context and confirmed capacity. | Separate branch; acknowledge request without implying acceptance. |
| Account or individual access grant | Later client materials or genuinely restricted tools | Login and manual sharing create more effort for both sides. | Defer for introductory PDFs. |
| Form-hosted fallback | Website route is unavailable during an approved pilot | Preserves capture if configured, disclosed and tested. | A fallback proposal; no tool choice or activation made. |

Do not give strangers access to CMS's internal Drive folder or client materials. Approved public exports should be isolated from internal source files. A reusable PDF does not need individual Drive sharing requests.

The existing owner-private CMS journey Site is not a prospect destination.

### Content on each resource page

Keep CMS's approved colors, Fraunces titles, Inter body text and Space Mono functional labels. The visitor sees the product and next action; implementation metadata stays internal.

1. Resource title and a plain-language purpose.
2. A preview of what they will receive; show the actual approved version.
3. A short scope statement, including whether it is a self-check or a human review.
4. The form immediately below the explanation.
5. A clear access button and delivery expectation.
6. Privacy information and a separate optional marketing choice.
7. Useful answers about download format, cost, login and scope.
8. An optional next step after access; never a compulsory sales call.

No sales popup, invented testimonial, countdown, fake scarcity or auto-selected subscription.

## 4. Form design and progressive qualification

### Resource form; minimum viable capture

| Field | Required? | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Email address | Yes for an email-gated resource | Delivery and contact record; validate syntax and delivery status. |
| First name | Optional | Friendly personalization; never block delivery if blank. |
| Business name | Optional | Early context; collect later if it creates friction. |
| Marketing subscription checkbox | Optional; unchecked | Separate permission for nurture emails. |
| Resource/source fields | Hidden | Resource ID, platform, post/campaign ID and landing-page version. |
| Privacy notice link | Visible | Explain the actual handling of submitted information. |

Do not require phone number, revenue, budget, employee count, account credentials or administrator access to obtain a simple worksheet.

For an email-gated offer, the post and DM must say that the resource page asks for an email. Do not advertise “instant direct download” and then introduce an undisclosed form.

### After access; optional qualification

Display: “What would you like this resource to help you decide?”

Offer choices: clarify the next step myself; get a focused second perspective; discuss help with implementation; just using the resource.

If someone requests help, collect the business/public page, their offer, role in the decision, one current problem and desired next step. Ask timing and investment context when paid support is being discussed. Request only the information needed for the next decision.

A business name or page URL helps establish context; it does not prove buying authority. A keyword does not establish fit.

### REVIEW form

Required: email, public page URL, service/business context, one goal and one question. Show scope and actual review availability before submission. Marketing permission remains separate and optional.

Confirmation must distinguish a received request from an accepted review. Publish a turnaround only after the reviewer and capacity are confirmed. If unavailable, disable intake or clearly offer an optional waitlist; do not silently collect requests nobody will handle.

## 5. Message drafting conventions

Every message below is a draft for the future working system. Statements such as “available,” “sent,” “submitted” and “stopped” are conditional on their corresponding verified action.

**Default initial message format:** One private reply with the keyword-specific body, the relevant access explanation and one website button. If an automation label is useful, show “CMS automated reply” once at the start; do not pretend an individual manually wrote an instant reply.

**Standard access explanation; append for gated resources:**  
“Open the page and enter your email to access the resource. Further marketing emails are optional.”

**Link notation:** {CLARITY_PAGE_URL}, {INQUIRY_PAGE_URL}, etc. are internal placeholders for verified public URLs. Never publish the placeholders.

**REVIEW explanation:**  
“The form requests the context needed for one focused observation. Submitting it is a request, not a confirmed review.”

**SAMPLE explanation:**  
“No form is needed to view the sample. You can request an email copy or further help if useful.”

The preferred simple flow has no scheduled Messenger chase after an external-link click. Qualification happens on the site, through an explicit reply or through consented email.

## 8. Public comment replies; acknowledge accurately

A public reply is optional and must follow a successful private-message action, not precede it.

- “The resource-page link is in your messages.”
- “Check your messages for the {RESOURCE_TITLE} request page.”
- “I have sent the request-page link privately.” Use “we” or an automated label if appropriate; avoid implying a personal response that did not occur.

If delivery was rejected or unavailable: “You can message the Page with {KEYWORD} to request the resource.” Only use if that direct-message route is working.

If status is uncertain, do not say “sent.” Use a supported fallback link or let the human reviewer handle it.

Do not post the recipient's email, business issue, fit classification or review answers in public. Do not ask them to comment more than once to force reach.

## 9. Optional conversational branch

A direct website link is the default. If CMS wants an in-platform conversation first, replace the website button in the initial private reply with a supported reply/continuation button, such as “Get the resource link.”

After a qualifying interaction, send:

“Here is the page for your {RESOURCE_TITLE}: {RESOURCE_PAGE_URL}. It asks for your email so you can access the resource and receive a copy. Additional marketing emails are optional.”

**Button:** Open resource page

Then stop unless the person explicitly asks a question or requests help. Do not add both a compulsory quick reply and a compulsory email form merely to inflate activity.

Do not treat “Get the resource link” as permission for a long nurture sequence. Use the channel's current delivery permissions and the recipient's request.

### If the person asks for help after receiving access

“What would you like help with?”

Suggested choices: understanding one part; applying it to my business; help with implementation; download problem.

Understanding: answer within the product boundary or invite a focused review.  
Application: ask the relevant optional question from Section 7.  
Implementation: collect the business goal and context, then route to the appropriate paid discussion.  
Download problem: resolve access first; do not insert a qualification pitch.

## 11. Lead states and nurturing logic

Store actual contact data in an authorized restricted lead workspace or CRM, not GitHub research files or a public register. No actual leads are created by this draft.

| State | Evidence needed | Allowed next design action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resource requester | Keyword request or form intent | Fulfill the request under available channel permissions. |
| Resource contact | Valid submission stored | Deliver the requested product; unknown fit remains unknown. |
| Access offered | Working resource returned | Invite optional context; not proof of reading. |
| Marketing pending | Subscription selected; confirmation not completed | Confirmation only; no nurture under the proposed double opt-in design. |
| Marketing subscriber | Recorded permission and required confirmation | Relevant nurture emails with unsubscribe. |
| Review requested | Explicit review form/context | Human eligibility/capacity check. |
| Review accepted | Reviewer confirms scope and availability | Deliver the bounded observation. |
| Qualified conversation | Business, authority/context, need and willingness to explore established | Discovery and appropriate scope discussion. |
| Proposal opportunity | A defined paid next step is under discussion | Proposal only after scope and fit. |
| Suppressed | Opt-out, hard bounce or other applicable suppression | Stop relevant promotional communication; no automatic resubscription. |

An owner who declines marketing can still request a review. A subscriber who has not asked for service should not be assigned a sales task just for opening an email.

Suggested internal fields: contact ID; normalized email; resource ID/version; requested timestamp; platform/post/campaign; form version; resource-access status; delivery email status; marketing choice; consent-text version/timestamp; confirmation timestamp; known business context; review state; human owner; next action; channel suppression.

A recorded interest in CLARITY is useful segmentation; it does not prove the business has a clarity problem.

## 13. CMS website handoff and measurement concept

Only the proposed architecture is documented. No code, credentials, protected-folder access, CRM connection, email provider connection or deployment is performed.

### Functional requirements for the developer

- One reusable resource landing-page and access-page layout.
- A resource catalog containing title, slug, version, file location, description and live/held status.
- One form that persists the request before reporting success.
- A working immediate download plus an independent resource-delivery email job.
- Separate marketing permission, confirmation and suppression handling.
- Optional context/review routing into a restricted workspace with an actual human owner.
- Clear error states, duplicate handling and support fallback.
- Mobile-friendly pages and tested access without CMS's signed-in owner session.

Do not introduce a “Create account” step unless there is an actual access-control need. A simple introductory worksheet should not require account onboarding.

### Attribution and identity

Carry non-sensitive campaign identifiers from the message link to the form; for example, utm_source=facebook, utm_medium=comment_dm, utm_campaign=cms_resource_clarity, utm_content={POST_CODE}.

UTM values are hints about attribution, not proof of the visitor's identity. Never place email addresses, names or sensitive answers in URLs. If CRM/contact linking is necessary, use an implementation-approved opaque reference and verify the association. An external link click must not be used to infer Messenger opt-in.

Maintain one contact with multiple resource interests rather than a new lead per keyword. Do not silently merge unrelated people from similar names or different social profiles.

### Observable funnel

Keyword requested; private reply sent; link clicked when measurable; page viewed when measurable; form submitted; access offered; delivery email sent/bounced; marketing confirmed; optional context submitted; review requested/accepted; paid opportunity.

“File opened,” “download completed” and “resource used” need independent evidence. Do not substitute message sends, opens or link clicks for these outcomes. Show missing data and known tracking limitations.

Useful ratios include form submissions / measured landing-page visitors and accepted review requests / known relevant contacts, using consistent windows and clear denominators. No conversion targets are assumed.

A website-button click does not opt the person into its Facebook contact flow or open the standard 24-hour messaging window; a supported interaction is needed. It also documents restrictions on Page-origin comments, groups and post types. [A1]

**Design implication:** Direct the initial reply to the website; use site/email follow-through. In-platform conversations are a separate tested branch.

### Instagram

The corresponding Instagram documentation distinguishes private replies, reply/button interactions and website clicks. It also documents initial-message and repeat-comment limits. [A2]

**Design implication:** Build a platform-specific flow; do not copy Facebook configuration assumptions.

### Email

Mailchimp distinguishes subscribed, non-subscribed and unsubscribed contacts. Its opt-in documentation states that audience signup settings do not automatically govern custom integrations/API behavior. [A3][A4]

**Design implication:** Store resource delivery and optional marketing permission separately; explicitly test custom-form behavior and the chosen provider. No Mailchimp subscription or purchase is assumed.

These are primary technical/vendor documents, not proof that CMS has configured these capabilities. 🟢 High for documented behavior; 🟡 Moderate for the proposed architecture's fit until implementation and buyer testing.
