**Prepared:** 14 October 2026; Asia/Manila  
**Scope:** 60 individual B-roll shot ideas and complete Google Flow-ready prompts; 10 collections, starter selection, editing combinations and generation guidance.  
**Status:** Draft production reference; prompts prepared, clips not generated or tested.  
**Audience:** Western owner-led expertise and high-value service businesses that have outgrown DIY or fragmented social marketing; secondary audiences retain their own context.  
**Approver:** Seul / Merille Nacino.  
**Authorization:** Requested shot/prompt development; standing instruction to upload CMS Markdown files to GitHub. This does not authorize publication, alteration of approved video assets or a change to the existing image-only Batch 02.

## Creative direction
Use recognizable actions that support CMS's explanation: opening a notebook, examining a source, arranging a useful asset, handing over a folder, clarifying a next step or correcting a small mistake.

Preserve the faceless approach through camera placement, not just a request for “no face.” Most prompts use hands and objects. Human heads stay outside the frame; glass, metal and device reflections must also be inspected.

Favor warm cream and ink, subdued slate or clay accents, natural light, matte materials and modest work environments. Keep prompts free of text; add exact approved copy, logos and narration in the editor. Generated work scenes remain illustrative and cannot establish actual CMS staff, offices, clients, delivery or results.

The related [background idea bank](./CMS_Stock_Photo_Background_Idea_Bank.md) provides still-image concepts. Its ideas are references, not proof that those images have been produced.

## Prompt approach and current Google guidance
Google's Veo prompt guide supports specifying framing, camera motion, style, lighting, location and action. Flow's help describes text, frame and reference inputs; prompts should complement supplied references. We adapt that guidance into **one main action and one camera instruction per B-roll shot**. This simplification is CMS production judgment, not a Google requirement.

The current Flow model-support page lists 4-, 6- and 8-second text/frame generations for the listed Veo 3.1 variants; ingredient availability and duration differ by variant. Confirm the active model and available controls before generation. Do not assume a feature available to one model is available to another.

Use vertical **9:16** for short vertical edits when available in the selected mode; use a separately composed landscape output when required. Google's January 2026 announcement documents native vertical output rolling out to Flow. Configure the actual ratio in Flow; prompt wording alone is not a substitute for the output setting.

## Suggested working settings
These are proposed creative defaults, not approved CMS technical standards or guaranteed model controls.

| Item | Starting recommendation |
|---|---|
| Generation duration | Choose 6 seconds for a simple action where the mode supports it; use an available 8-second option for slower actions or modes requiring it. |
| Final edit duration | Select the cleanest roughly 2–4-second portion; longer holds can support slower narration. |
| Framing | Vertical 9:16 for vertical content; keep important action in the middle/lower working area without assuming a universal platform safe zone. |
| Camera | Locked camera for hand/object actions; a short slow slide or push-in for still-life or empty-room shots. |
| Action | One purposeful action; let the object settle afterward. |
| Lighting | Natural window light or a believable soft desk lamp; consistent within a sequence. |
| Text zone | A plain wall, tabletop or paper area that stays quiet throughout the clip. Verify contrast frame by frame. |
| Audio | The prompts request room ambience without speech or music. Remove generated audio in editing when a clean silent background is needed. |
| Reference workflow | Reuse a suitable start frame or supported ingredient to help retain the same setting, props and sleeves. Inspect continuity; references do not guarantee an exact match. |
| Fallback | If a physical interaction is poor, use a still-life with camera movement or begin after the difficult interaction has already occurred. |

## Continuity brief
Use one approved reference image for a particular workspace sequence where practical. Preserve its desk material, window direction, cup, notebook, sleeves and prop colors. Use the same neutral clothing across linked hand shots. Avoid combining clearly different hands as though they belong to one continuous action.

Optional founder-style scenes can use a faceless adult Filipina stand-in with cream sleeves when that fits the approved creative brief. This is an illustrative casting choice; it must not be described as actual Merille footage. Owner-viewpoint clips may use suitable diverse adult hands without inferring business maturity from demographics.

Create single clips independently, then assemble them in the editor. A request for a series in one prompt can introduce transitions, changing props or an unwanted face reveal. Exact camera paths, durations, silence and identity restrictions are intentions to inspect, not guarantees.

## Camera terms in plain language
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Locked / stationary | The camera stays in one position; only the subject moves. |
| Overhead | The camera looks straight down at the table. |
| Close-up / detail | A small action or object fills the meaningful part of the frame. |
| Slow push-in | The camera moves slightly closer to a subject. |
| Slow lateral slide | The camera moves a short distance sideways. |
| Waist-height shot | The camera is positioned to show the action below the person's shoulders. |

## Editing and asset notes
- Add exact copy, CMS logo and any approved narration after generation; preserve the brand's font roles and writing standard.
- Keep essential overlays away from the actual platform interface after checking the current preview.
- Avoid text over moving hands, paper folds or busy screens. Check readability against the changing background, not just one still frame.
- Gentle straight cuts or a restrained transition usually suffice. Use transitions to clarify the thought rather than imitate a required carousel formula.
- Match light, scale and camera angle when cutting between related actions. A cut does not need to pretend two unrelated generated spaces are one real consultation.
- Keep clean raw clips alongside edited versions; retain generation provenance and do not remove required provider marks.
- Suggested filename: CMS_BR-A01_Open_Notebook_9x16_v01.mp4. A revised generation increments the version; approval is recorded for the actual exported clip.
- Suggested metadata: shot ID, prompt version, model/mode, ratio, generation length, reference source, generation date, review outcome, content link and approval scope. Use the existing register rather than creating a competing live tracker.

## Clip review
Inspect the whole generated clip before selecting it:
1. Faces, partial facial profiles, heads entering the crop or facial reflections.
2. Fingers, wrist shapes and the way a hand touches an object.
3. Paper, cups, cards, cables and furniture changing shape or multiplying.
4. The camera drifting into a face, screen, cluttered text zone or an unwanted setting.
5. Generated readable words, invented metrics, interface brands or customer details.
6. Unexpected speech, music, voices or other audio unsuitable for the intended edit.
7. The actual output ratio, edge crops and consistency across the chosen sequence.
8. Whether the image is clearly illustrative where viewers could mistake it for real CMS staff, client activity or verified results.

Reject or regenerate a faulty shot; a promising thumbnail does not establish full-clip quality. A passed visual check is not automatically publication approval.
