## 3. The foundation

**FOUNDATION / ESTABLISHED**

Clarity before commitment.

| Name | Meaning and role |
| --- | --- |
| Cloud | Infrastructure; a flexible network of specialized resources and talent, brought together as needed. |
| Mind | Strategy; psychology informed thinking that diagnoses the business need and directs the work. |
| Social | Execution and relationship; human partnership and the credible social presence built through it. |

### Vision

To be the trusted bridge that turns every business’s idea into a brand people know, trust, and choose; no matter their stage, generation, or culture.

### Mission

We help business owners grow with confidence by matching the right depth of social media strategy and execution to where their business, and they personally, actually are.

### Core belief

We believe a business shouldn’t have to guess what it needs. Diagnosis first, investment second; so every investment actually moves you forward.

### Sage + Caregiver

Sage makes the reasoning clear. Caregiver protects the client’s interests, including recommending less when less is appropriate.

**Values:** Right-Sized Help; Diagnosis Before Prescription; Partnership Over Vendorship; Shared Growth. These values are decision filters for scope, specialist matching, communication, and ongoing recommendations.


## 4. Who the identity serves

**AUDIENCE / ESTABLISHED + APPLICATION**

A business that has outgrown disconnected effort.

Growth-Stage, Owner-Led Businesses That Have Outgrown DIY / Fragmented Social Media Marketing

**Commercial priority:** Western clients first; Philippine clients second. Business maturity and the marketing problem lead qualification.

### The perception we want to earn

“They understand what is happening in my business. They can explain what deserves attention, who should handle it, and why.” This is the proposed audience response guiding design decisions; it is not a verified customer quotation.

| Identity requirement | How to express it |
| --- | --- |
| Understanding | Name the business situation specifically before introducing services. |
| Competence | Show the diagnosis, reasoning, responsibilities, and relevant evidence. |
| Right-sized support | Explain the recommended scope and its limits without implying that more is always better. |
| Human accountability | Identify real contributors and what each person is responsible for. |

### Adapt the explanation; preserve the identity

The existing nine generation and culture personas are communication variations, not nine equal commercial markets. Ask what the individual buyer needs to evaluate a decision; do not assume their budget, trust style, or sophistication from age or nationality.

Use English as the default. Use Tagalog or Taglish when it fits the actual Philippine audience and context. Keep the same standards for clarity, proof, and respect in every language.


## 5. Voice and language

**VOICE / ESTABLISHED + APPLICATION**

Confident. Authoritative. Direct. Empathetic.

Speak as a relatable partner. Confidence comes from explaining the recommendation and its basis. Empathy comes from recognizing the owner’s actual pressure and respecting the decision they need to make.

| Trait | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confident | Here is what we recommend and why. | Unsupported certainty or guaranteed results. |
| Authoritative | This finding changes the priority because… | Claims of expertise without a reason or evidence. |
| Direct | The message is unclear about who the service helps. | Vague criticism or elaborate corporate phrasing. |
| Empathetic | You have been keeping this running alongside the rest of the business. | Shaming an owner for doing the work themselves. |

### Writing rules

**Audience-content scope:** Follow the approved [CMS Content Writing Standard](./CMS_Content_Writing_Standard.md), including educational content and its Grounded human perspective rule. Fixed writing sequences (including situation, implication, next step), layout recipes, and evidence structures in this guide are internal planning guidance; they do not prescribe audience sentence, paragraph, or carousel progression. Internal documents and separately scoped utility assets retain structured formats. Locked identity wording and voice remain intact; production and publication remain separately gated.


- Use concrete nouns and active verbs. Explain specialist terms the first time they matter.
- Lead with the situation, then the implication, then the recommended next step.
- Use “we” for agency commitments; name the person responsible when discussing delivery.
- Keep headings short. Use sentence case for headlines and body text; uppercase is for brief labels.
- Use periods and semicolons naturally. Limit italics to one emphasized phrase in a composition.

### Words and habits to exclude

Synergy, 10x, secret formula, guru, ninja, rockstar, unmatched, revolutionary, results guaranteed, and hustle culture slogans. Avoid cold phrases such as “our clients leverage our solutions.”

**Boundary:** A process commitment can be definite; a commercial outcome must be supported and appropriately qualified. Do not imply existing specialist capacity, client results, or delivery systems that CMS cannot demonstrate.


## 6. Messaging architecture

**MESSAGING / ESTABLISHED + APPLICATION**

Recognition earns attention. Reasoning earns trust.

Why → Belief → How → What → Proof → CTA

Use this hierarchy across the communication sequence. A single asset may carry one part of it; do not force all six stages into every layout.

| Funnel stage | Communication task | Proposed CTA pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Help an owner recognize a specific problem or question. | Consider this question / save a useful explanation. |
| Consideration | Show how discovery, diagnosis, and specialist matching work. | Explore the approach / discuss the situation. |
| Decision | Explain scope, responsibilities, relevant proof, and what happens next. | Request a conversation / review the recommendation. |
| Retention and advocacy | Explain progress, constraints, and the next justified decision. | Review priorities / agree the next step. |

### Four established narrative pillars

- Right-Sized, Not One-Size: connect the recommended depth of help to the need.
- Specialists Over Generalists: explain who owns the specific task and why.
- Guidance Before the Sell: make discovery and diagnosis visible before a recommendation.
- A System, Not a Solo Act: show strategic oversight across the specialist network.

### Naming and phrase hierarchy

**Brand name:** Cloud Mind Social. Introduce the full name before using CMS in external material. **Descriptor:** Strategic Social Intelligence. **Core idea:** Make the digital world make sense. **Operating principle:** Diagnosis Before Prescription.

The approved tagline is “We build the bridge from unknown to impact.” CTA wording must match the actual destination and availability; do not imply a free audit or a guaranteed diagnosis on every first call.


## 7. Creative direction

**DIRECTION APPROVED / EXECUTION PROPOSED**

Structured thinking. Editorial expression. Human warmth.

Make the value of CMS’s thinking visible. Use typography and spacing to establish the argument; use diagrams and annotations when they improve understanding; use people and language to make accountability tangible.

| Expression | Visual characteristics | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial clarity | Warm cream, strong Fraunces headline, readable Inter explanation, generous margins, one focal point. | The reader needs to understand a principle or question. |
| Diagnostic structure | Numbered stages, labeled observations, comparison panels, simple connections, a clear reading order. | The reader needs to understand the reasoning or process. |
| Human partnership | Real people, named responsibilities, conversational language, selective coral accents. | The reader needs to understand who is involved and how the relationship works. |

### One identity across all three

Keep color roles, type roles, alignment, and spacing consistent. Vary the arrangement to fit the communication task. These are expressions of one brand, not separate sub-brands or content pillars.

### Editorial does not mean decorative

A beautiful headline alone cannot demonstrate Strategic Social Intelligence. Support it with a useful observation, clearly labeled reasoning, or relevant proof. Avoid ornamental layouts that could belong to any lifestyle brand.

### Working visual rules

- Use one main message and one dominant visual anchor.
- Use coral or ochre selectively; do not make both compete for attention.
- Prefer flat color, purposeful lines, and simple panels over decorative effects.
- Give whitespace a job: separate ideas and make the order of reading clear.


## 8. Color system

**PALETTE LOCKED / USAGE PROPOSED**

Five colors. Defined roles.

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloud | Mist slate · #AEB8B4 | Supporting backgrounds, dividers, and network graphics; use sparingly. |
| Mind | Warm ink · #1C1B1A | Primary text, headlines, diagnostic copy, and strong contrast. |
| Social | Clay coral · #D6553A | Relationship touchpoints, specialist emphasis, and CTA accents. |
| Social rotation | Deep ochre · #C98A2E | A second Social accent for variation within the same family. |
| Shared base | Warm cream · #F7F2EA | The default surface supporting all three brand ideas. |

### Proposed application discipline

Let cream dominate ordinary editorial pages. Use ink to carry the information. Use slate for structure and one Social accent to direct attention. An ink background can create emphasis if the text remains readable.

Do not require every color in every composition. Do not assign a different palette to each audience, platform, or generation. Do not define Phase 3 content pillar colors before those pillars are approved.

Keep source hex values exact. Avoid arbitrary opacity changes on text. Any tint must serve a supporting purpose and pass a fresh contrast check.

**Reference discrepancy:** the PDF’s handoff page also mentions #8A8981 for labels. It is not part of its five-color palette table. Do not add it as a sixth brand color by default; record a decision if it is retained.


## 9. Readable color pairings

**ACCESSIBILITY / PROPOSED CORRECTIONS**

Preserve the palette. Check the application.

Use a minimum contrast target of 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for large text. Large text means at least 18 pt regular or 14 pt bold at the viewed size. For social graphics that shrink on screen, default to 4.5:1 for essential text. [S5]

| Pairing | Ratio* | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm ink / warm cream | 15.43:1 | Suitable for ordinary text. Default pairing. |
| Warm ink / mist slate | 8.45:1 | Suitable for ordinary text on a supporting panel. |
| Warm ink / deep ochre | 5.86:1 | Suitable for ordinary text on an ochre accent. |
| Warm ink / clay coral | 4.27:1 | Below the ordinary-text target; large text only. |
| Warm cream / clay coral | 3.61:1 | Large text only; insufficient for small CTA labels. |
| Warm cream / deep ochre | 2.63:1 | Do not use for essential text. |
| Mist slate / warm cream | 1.83:1 | Decoration only; do not use for essential text. |

*Computed from the exact hex values using sRGB relative luminance; displayed ratios are rounded. Passing decisions use the unrounded calculation. Pair order does not change the ratio.

### Proposed correction to the reference CTA

The reference specifies cream text on coral buttons. That pairing does not reach 4.5:1. For small CTAs, propose cream text on an ink fill, with coral used as a nearby accent. This changes application, not the locked palette; approve it explicitly before treating it as a new locked rule.

The reference’s #8A8981 label color on cream is approximately 3.15:1; use ink for small labels in this draft. Do not communicate a distinction through color alone. Pair colors with words, shapes, or labels; keep text off busy photographs.


## 10. Typography system

**TYPEFACES LOCKED / SIZE RULES PROPOSED**

Let the hierarchy do the work.

| Role | Established typeface | Weights and treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headlines | Fraunces | 500 Medium and 600 Semibold; regular plus italic. |
| Body copy | Inter | 400 Regular and 500 Medium. |
| Labels and microcopy | Space Mono | 400 Regular and 700 Bold; brief uppercase labels with tracking. |

### Proposed starting sizes for documents

At Letter or A4 portrait size: cover title 36–44 pt; page heading 26–32 pt; section heading 15–18 pt; body 11–12 pt; labels 9–10 pt. Increase sizes for presentations and social layouts; judge the exported result at its actual viewing size.

### Composition rules

- Use Fraunces for the main thought, Inter for explanation, and Space Mono for short navigational information.
- Reserve Fraunces italic for one emphasized phrase per composition. Avoid full italic paragraphs.
- Use left alignment for sustained reading. Aim for 45–75 characters per body line and approximately 1.35–1.5 line spacing.
- Avoid all-caps paragraphs, stretched type, artificial bolding, and decorative letter spacing in body text.

**Implementation check:** confirm the actual families and weights in Canva. Do not substitute silently if a font is unavailable. The PDF handoff mentions Inter 600 for buttons while its primary body table lists 400/500; this guide uses Inter 500 until that extra weight is confirmed.


## 11. Layout and graphic language

**APPLICATION / PROPOSED**

Make the order of thinking visible.

### The basic composition

Begin with a small context label, then the main headline, then the explanation or evidence, then a clear next step where appropriate. A signature or page number should support orientation without competing with the main idea.

| Element | Proposed rule |
| --- | --- |
| Margins | Start with 7–9% of the short edge for documents; reserve additional space where platform controls or crops may overlap. |
| Grid | Use a single reading column or two clearly related columns. Align headings, panels, and captions to shared edges. |
| Spacing | Use a consistent family of spacing increments. Increase the gap between sections beyond the gap within a section. |
| Panels | Use flat fills and restrained corners. Every panel should group a meaningful unit of information. |
| Lines and nodes | Use only when they show a real relationship, sequence, or annotation; label the relationship when needed. |
| Emphasis | Use scale, weight, position, or an accent selectively. Do not emphasize every line. |

### Reusable layout recipes

- Insight page: question or claim; supporting explanation; one practical implication.
- Diagnostic comparison: two clearly labeled situations; evidence or criteria; interpretation.
- Process page: a small number of named stages with responsibilities and outputs.
- Specialist page: real portrait or approved asset; role; contribution; how the work is directed.

These are layout recipes for later templates, not approved posts or a replacement for content architecture. Avoid generic network globes, decorative dashboards, unnecessary arrows, and dense walls of small text.


## 12. Logo and brand signature

**LOGO / OPEN DECISION**

Use an approved master before defining precision rules.

The reviewed repository still marks the final logo direction as in development. This guide does not treat an earlier concept, generated image, or recent extraction as the approved master without a recorded decision.

| Asset to confirm | Required purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Primary horizontal signature | General brand identification where width is available. |
| Secondary stacked signature | Compact layouts with more vertical space. |
| Symbol or avatar | Small profile and icon applications, only after recognition and crop tests. |
| Monochrome and reversed variants | Reliable reproduction across light and dark backgrounds. |
| Master formats | An editable vector master plus appropriate transparent raster exports. |

### Rules that can apply now

- Keep proportions intact. Do not stretch, skew, crop, redraw, or add effects to an approved asset.
- Place the asset on a clear background with sufficient contrast. Use only approved color variants.
- Do not add an unapproved tagline to a logo lockup.
- Do not rebuild the logo by typing the brand name in a similar font.

### Rules that need the actual master

Define clear space from a measurable feature of the final mark. Set minimum sizes through small-screen and print testing. Check horizontal, stacked, circular crop, grayscale, and reversed applications. Record the smallest size that remains recognizable; do not invent a universal pixel minimum.

Until the master is confirmed, plain “Cloud Mind Social” text may identify this guideline document. It is a document title or attribution, not a newly approved wordmark.


## 13. People, imagery, and motion

**APPLICATION / PROPOSED**

Show the humans behind the work.

### Photography

Prioritize real specialists, founders, working sessions, and relevant business environments. Use natural light, balanced skin tones, uncluttered compositions, and a consistent crop approach. Leave clear space for adjacent text; do not place small text over faces or detailed backgrounds.

| Choose | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| A real specialist with a name, role, and relevant contribution. | Stock teams presented as CMS employees or fictional client portraits. |
| Real working materials that illustrate the method and are cleared for use. | Screens full of invented metrics or confidential client information. |
| Simple, labeled illustrations that explain a relationship. | Literal cloud/brain/social-icon collages used as interchangeable decoration. |
| A restrained visual treatment that preserves the subject. | Heavy filters, artificial luxury cues, or effects that compete with meaning. |

### Illustration and icons

Use one coherent icon family per composition. Match stroke weight and corner behavior. Prefer abstract relationships and purposeful diagrams over stock technology symbolism. Supporting graphics must not be mistaken for the brand’s final logo.

### Motion and video

Reveal information in the order it should be understood. Use simple cuts, short fades, or restrained movement. Keep captions readable and within the platform’s current safe area. Important information must remain understandable with sound off; offer a static version where motion is unnecessary.

### Authenticity and permission

Use images with suitable rights and consent. Label illustrative or AI-generated scenes when a viewer could mistake them for CMS staff, clients, or results. Human warmth should come from actual relationships and useful language, not fabricated evidence.


## 14. Proof and information design

**EVIDENCE / ESTABLISHED PRINCIPLE + APPLICATION**

A claim should carry its context.

### Proof card structure

Situation → Intervention → Observed result → Time period → Source → Limitations

Name the metric and unit. Include the baseline, measurement window, relevant platform or data source, and the scope of CMS’s contribution. Distinguish observed change from a claim that CMS caused it.

| Evidence practice | Guideline |
| --- | --- |
| Research confidence | High: strong, relevant primary evidence. Moderate: useful evidence with meaningful limits. Anecdotal: illustrative experience, not a general finding. Unverified: excluded from findings. Apply the full Track A methodology when producing research. |
| Research status | Track A drafts are an evidence layer pending human review. Do not quietly convert their findings into locked audience or brand decisions. |
| Client results | Use only documented and permitted results. Identify the measurement period, baseline, source, and relevant constraints. |
| Testimonials | Use authentic, authorized wording. Do not invent a client name, role, quotation, or endorsement. |

### Charts and comparisons

Label units, dates, comparison groups, and sources directly. Use ink for essential labels. Use coral or ochre to highlight one relevant comparison; add words or symbols so color is not the only signal. Avoid decorative 3D effects, unexplained percentages, or selective time windows.

### When commercial proof is not yet available

Show the process, a clearly labeled worked example, or the questions used in diagnosis. Keep sample data visibly labeled. A polished layout must never make a hypothesis, mockup, or aspiration appear to be an achieved result.

Native kit setup is a separate implementation step; the available connection in this session does not expose native Brand Kit creation or editing. Do not label the kit as configured merely because the guideline document exists.


## 17. Approval and quality control

**GOVERNANCE / PROPOSED**

Make every approval specific.

| Decision | Current state / next action |
| --- | --- |
| Brand foundation and audience | Preserve the established repository decisions. |
| Creative direction | Approved by Seul in this conversation on September 15, 2026. |
| Application standards in this guide | Version 0.1 recommendations; review before marking them locked. |
| Logo master and tagline | Confirm the assets and final wording separately. |
| Contrast and reference discrepancies | Decide CTA pairing, small-label treatment, extra gray, and Inter 600 usage. |
| Canva kit identity and setup | Confirm the correct kit and implement the approved asset set. |

### Preflight for every new brand asset

- Does it address one clear audience need and communication task?
- Does the language sound confident, direct, and empathetic?
- Are the colors exact and their roles intentional? Are fonts and weights correct?
- Can essential text be read at the actual viewing size? Are contrast and crop safe?
- Does the layout show a clear reading order and an appropriate next step?
- Are names, images, quotations, and results authentic and cleared for use?
- Are claims sourced, time-bounded where relevant, and honest about limitations?
- Is the logo an approved asset, and is any tagline actually finalized?
- Does the asset respect the current phase and approval status?

### Change control

Record what changed, why, who approved it, and when. Seul approves changes to locked identity decisions. Keep the source guide, approved assets, and Canva implementation aligned. Archive superseded versions instead of mixing them into the active set.
